#  Deep-sea rifts and forbidden zones for life

In the center of the endless sea lies a dark chasm that stretches across the ocean, like a wound cleaved by a giant sword. Legend has it that this is the mark left by the fingernail of a god who fell during Ragnarok—as that god lay dying, he tried to grasp something, and his fingernail scratched the earth, creating this crack that can never heal.

The area beneath the rift is called the Abyss, the closest point in the Phenomenon Realm to the Mirror Realm. The Abyss is inhabited by bizarre monsters—thousand-legged sea serpents, octopuses with human faces, and transparent whales that slowly digest entire fleets in the deep sea. These monsters possess high concentrations of evil spiritual power, their very existence polluting the surrounding waters. The ocean has thus become a forbidden zone; fishermen dare only cast their nets near the shore, chanting ancestral incantations. But some desperate individuals still sail their small boats toward the rift—it is said that some have seen light deep within it, not the phosphorescence of monsters, but a pure, rhythmic radiance, like a heartbeat.

Was it the still-beating heart of a god? Or some artifact left behind by Ragnarok? No one knows. Those who went there never returned, and those who returned were no longer "human."

The Forbidden Zones of Life are abysses on land. Born from the Day of Decay, each of these zones buries tens of thousands of lives. The land there is black, the air thick with the sweet stench of decay, and any living thing that enters feels its spiritual energy rapidly draining away, as if the earth itself is devouring it. Within these Forbidden Zones dwell Annihilation-level evil spirits—the kings of evil spirits, possessing power equivalent to, or even greater than, that of a human at the peak of the Dominator Realm.

Why didn't the forbidden zone of life continue to expand? Because of the heroic spirits. Those who sacrificed themselves on the day of decay became the souls of the dead on the earth through sacrifice. They gave up reincarnation, gave up rest, and slumber beneath the earth as spirits, suppressing evil spirits with their immense willpower. The existence of heroic spirits is a silent price—they can no longer speak, no longer think, no longer possess self-awareness, but are merely pure energy barriers. Yet they still protect.

Each boundary marker bears at least one tragedy.

The psionic Great Wall on the border is humanity's front line against demonic invasion. Forged from psionic energy, it ceaselessly draws mana from the border region to sustain itself. Every few miles along the wall stands a watchtower, guarded by volunteers—they know they are on the front lines, know they will surely die if demons launch a large-scale invasion, yet they stand there nonetheless. Not out of bravery, but because behind them are families , land, and everything that should not be trampled by demons. Those who carry the torch for others must not be allowed to freeze to death in the snow.

However, evil spirits and evil gods can never be completely eradicated. Wherever there is life, there will be conflict and malice; where there is malice, evil spirits will breed. The people's tireless efforts to build the Great Wall, cultivate monks, and cleanse the oceans were merely delaying the inevitable end. This is not a story about victory, but a story about choosing to fight even in a war destined for defeat.

The most endearing thing about the children of humanity is that there will always be those naive adults who believe in the light. They strive relentlessly and unscrupulously to become deities, hoping to save the world, only to find that the end is becoming the merciless Way of Heaven. Before becoming the Way, they separated their last bit of goodness, letting it manifest as spirits and be born into the human world. Because they are part of the Way of Heaven, their souls possess the quality of immortality—they do not perish, and can be reincarnated infinitely, retaining their memories. This is the last act of mercy that the Way of Heaven leaves for the world. Although it observes coldly, and may even have lost all emotion, the "human" before becoming the Way of Heaven, before completely dissipating, left behind that seed. Those seeds are scattered among the human world, sometimes as a child lost in thought on the street, sometimes as a soldier protecting the weak in war, sometimes as a traveler lost in the wilderness yet still moving forward. You don't know who they are—they themselves don't know who they are either. But when the world truly needs a ray of light, they will remember.
