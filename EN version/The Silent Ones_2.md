# The Silent Ones

Lin Jing disliked talking to people, and unless absolutely necessary, he had no interest in listening to other people's idle chatter. If no one asked him to, he might not utter a single word all day. Even when he did speak, it was only in the form of written reasoning, not casual conversation. Lin Jing wasn't a pragmatist who rejected speaking; he simply felt that most conversations had become a social tool rather than a means of exchanging information, and that speaking as a means of communication was foolish and inefficient. Moreover, speaking always meant obtaining information from living people; if you lived in the wilderness with no one to talk to, would you not survive? Aren't books and natural things more important than speaking?
