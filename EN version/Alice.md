#  Alice

“Alice, we don’t belong to any organization or interest group,” the old man sighed.

Alice simply smiled, a serene and peaceful smile that showed no trace of the hardships that had etched upon her.

"For many years, I have been running away from that monster, deformed and ugly, like a deep wind tunnel that reveals the bones, and it keeps echoing in my dreams, when I am alone, when the stars have dimmed and everything is silent."

The old man paused for a moment, then said, "We are both ancient holy spirits. Is there no way to eliminate that monster? You wield destruction, capable of easily destroying a city with a mere gesture. You are kind-hearted, and the people you created support and love you. You have become a true legend, and future generations will praise your name."

Alice's expression remained unchanged, as if she were simply listening to something objectively true.

She gently raised her hand, looked up at the sky, as if recalling a distant past.

"Yes, I have tried all sorts of ways to destroy this monster, but my body is already pure white, while it is invisible and elusive, as if only I can see it."

She closed her eyes, her fingertips trembling slightly, like an ancient statue standing silently at the end of time, isolated from the world.
 
Space began to distort, mountains became blurry shadows, lightning exploded high in the sky, and a ghostly blue light flickered like a pulse, illuminating Alice's calm to the point of indifference.

“Did you see it?” she whispered. “That monster, it’s been waiting for me.”

The old man had never seen a deity like this before—neither angry, nor fearful, nor even sorrowful. There was only one kind of... extreme clarity, like the moment after lingering on the edge of a cliff a thousand times, finally choosing to jump—a resolute determination that penetrates to the very marrow of one's bones.

"On that fateful day, I briefly lost my great power, and even lowly monsters were able to bully me."

"I fell amidst the wind and dust, my clothes no longer pristine. I had to eat and excrete like a mortal, the trivialities of flesh and blood beginning to consume me. Demons bared their fierce fangs at me, leaders and lords revealed bloodthirsty smiles, while the rulers coldly watched me fall from my pedestal. Only my kin died one by one before my eyes to protect me. I felt as if I had become that weak and helpless girl again in the face of fear and injustice, driven mad with powerlessness, letting tears fill my eyes."

"Something burst forth from my soul—it was that monster. It relentlessly pounced on the wicked crowd and tore them to pieces."

Alice's body trembled slightly, and a golden tear rolled down her tightly closed eyes. This tear was not a sign of weakness, retreat, escape, or melancholy—but rather a sign of relief, redemption, and reconciliation with another version of herself.

She slowly walked towards the monster, which had long since stopped roaring, howling, and struggling; it simply looked at her.

"It was covered in blood and tattered, but its eyes... were so clean and clear, just like... me a long time ago."

These are all serendipitous achievements, perfectly natural creations.

Alice murmured, "It's you... So it was you."

It was herself, the one who was abandoned, the one who cried alone at night, the one who longed to be understood and comforted, but could only hide in the darkness in solitude.

The monster's body gradually shrank, its form dissipating and merging into the wind, the flames, the land, and the stars.

Standing before it, she felt as if she had reclaimed her lost soul.

She gently opened her arms and hugged the self that had been forgotten for far too long.

On its battle-worn body, I saw a pair of clear eyes that were remarkably similar to mine. It turned out that it was me, the me who was stuck in the past, the little me who was overlooked because I was too slow, the me who chose silence in battle after battle, lost my voice in glory after glory, and gradually lost the sense of "who I am" in victory after victory.

I have been advancing in glory, while it is the lingering spirit in that corner, waiting for redemption day after day for thousands of years.

It cries in the dead of night, roars in its dreams, and even when everything has been forgotten, it still crouches in a dark corner, waiting to be seen.

That's me... the me who didn't want to grow up.

The world seemed to stand still, as if caught in a silent vortex for a moment. The clouds high in the sky condensed into a gigantic face, expressionless, yet sending shivers down the spines of all who gazed upon it.

The grass and trees on the ground were withered and yellow, fading and cracking inch by inch, as if this space was being annihilated.

Ishgard, you are the king of dragons, how could you care about the tiny resilience of an ant?

Heavens, heavens are weeping.

Alice stood there, her snow-white hair partially obscuring her face, but unable to hide her bright eyes.

Heaven watched silently.

“It’s not the enemy,” she murmured.

The monster crawled with difficulty, its body covered in wounds from the battle, each tearing through its flesh. Yet its eyes showed no hostility, instead revealing an extremely complex, almost sorrowful emotion.

You deserve a better life, even if you are covered in curses and filth.

"Woo," the monster horned, moving forward like a child longing to go home, stretching out its tiny hand.

"Woo!" The monster let out a mournful cry as its paws were pierced by thorns that suddenly sprouted from the ground. It stopped in a daze, its eyes still holding the lingering joy from before. Its hands froze in mid-air, and its eyes gradually lost their luster. There was no hostility in them, but they gradually became wooden, like a walking corpse.

“It’s you…” Alice stepped forward, her movements gentle, and approached it cautiously.

Its towering body was covered with fragments of broken memories, as if pieced together from images extracted from countless nightmares.

"You've always been by my side."

Alice paused, her voice becoming heavy and hoarse.

"You were born the first time I was mocked, the first time I was abandoned, the first time I wanted to cry but had to force a smile...

The last sentence sounded like the mournful cry of a wild beast.

"Woo..." The monster suddenly stopped, its low, hoarse whimper echoing through the air with intense, overwhelming emotion.

At this moment, it is like a child who has been away from home for too long, timidly waiting for its mother's hand to reach out.

"You... are me."

"You are the me who was forced to mature, the me who had to grit my teeth and survive, the me who... pretended in the light and went crazy at night."

She slowly approached it, extending her jade-like hands covered in scars, as if stepping into a reconciliation that had been awaited for a thousand years.

At this moment, the priests watched from afar, while only one young apprentice murmured, "What...what is she doing? That's clearly a Soul Eater...born from chaos, taking pleasure in destruction and annihilation."

“No.” The old man closed his eyes and whispered, “That’s not just a demon, it’s also… herself.”

"I know you hate it."

"I know you want to destroy the world and tear all those who have hurt us to pieces."

"But you just want to be understood, right?"

The monster stirred, and a tiny point of light appeared in its eye, trembling in the wind like a tear about to go out of a candle.

Alice reached out, through the chaos, and placed her hand on the monster's forehead.

"This is a belated understanding, just as you once understood me."

To escape it is to escape my original sin, my pain, and the reality I have never dared to face. Yet, I have reconciled with this version of myself.

In that instant, the heavens and earth shook, and countless bolts of lightning exploded in the sky. The monster roared, its voice filled with pain, struggle, relief, and trembling. Its body slowly dissolved, transforming into countless streams of light that pierced through Alice's body and flowed into the depths of her soul.

Alice suddenly opened her eyes! In her pupils, there was only a pale gold, but now there was a deep purplish-black light. It was the color of the abyss, the color of pain, the color of no longer running away.

She didn't fall; instead, she stood even more firmly.

"I am no longer perfect."

“I am no longer that aloof, otherworldly Holy Spirit.”

"But I am finally... complete."

Later, Alice relinquished her divine position and ceased relying on external faith. Instead, she traveled far and wide, using her powers to help those in need. She endured hardship and was often covered in filth, yet her soul seemed to radiate light.

We must acknowledge that individual power is limited. While the power of deities is immense, it is built upon the exploitation of the inhuman. Deities are indifferent to the joys and sorrows of mortals, for they are already beings even more inhuman than spiritual practitioners.

Because to become a god you need to maintain your noble image, you cannot mingle with mortals, and your thinking speed and knowledge are not on the same level.

He became a god, but he was not happy and always wanted more.
