#  Pluto

The underworld has no time or space, no concepts, no living beings, only endless nothingness.

At first, He did not know what He was. The darkness was filled with points of light, which floated slowly and aimlessly. He was the only one among them that could "see." He had no eyes, but He had a direction—towards the points of light, towards the boundless darkness beneath Him, towards some "external" that He Himself could not explain.

He tried to touch one of the points of light.

In that instant, something like a tsunami surged in. A wheat field, a summer breeze, a woman's hand brushing against the tips of wheat ears, laughter, and then nothing—that memory burst like a bubble, dissipating into the darkness. He withdrew his "hand," the point of light still hovering there, as if nothing had ever happened, but something seemed to be burning in His consciousness, leaving a faint warmth.

He touched another. This one was cold. Thick fog, the sticky sound of horses' hooves pounding in the mud, a man's curse before he fell, then darkness. Another. Screams, firelight, a baby's cry, the muffled sound of metal being chopped. Yet another. Someone was humming a song, a very soft tune, with the sound of waves lapping against the shore in the background. Yet another. Nothing at all, pure emptiness, an emptiness that wasn't even darkness, and the moment He touched it, He felt Himself being sucked in, and He quickly retreated.

He stopped and began to think.

Who is this intimately connected little thinker?

Within those fragments of light are people, voices, warmth, colors, and stories. But none of those stories belong to Him. He is the subject that "touches," the subject that "sees."

Ah, it's me! I see myself! It turns out it's me all along.

But no one called His name, no hand touched His face, no wind blew across His non-existent body. He searched for traces of "me" in those fragments of memory, searching for a corner where He could find refuge, searching for a very, very long time—in His perception, it was almost eternal.

No.

He exists beyond all memory. Was He there before these points of light were born? Or did He exist because of them? He frantically searched, touching each point of light one by one, countless fragments flooding into His consciousness—some dancing at harvest time, some dying on their deathbeds, some seeing snow for the first time, some seeing the sun for the last time. Landslides, tsunamis, the first cry of a newborn baby, the last sigh of an old person before death. He swallowed it all, like something that had been thirsting for infinitely long suddenly tasting something, unable to stop.

Then He couldn't tell anymore.

In that instant, He was simultaneously the dancer and the dying; simultaneously seeing the snow and the last sun. A landslide exploded within His spine, a tsunami flooded His lungs—but He had neither spine nor lungs. Those sensations tore at each other, ripping Him into pieces and then kneading them back together. He screamed, but the sound did not travel far; it merely reverberated within this sea of light, absorbed, devoured, and dissolved by countless points of light.

He was tired.

After calming down, He found He felt something inexplicable about those specks of light. Anger? No, they were innocent. Jealousy? Perhaps. Each of these fragments had once lived, each had once had a "self," while He had nothing. He couldn't even say what He "once" was, because even the concept of "once" was picked up from those fragments.

He began to observe the flow of the points of light. They slowly converged in one direction, where there was a shadow darker than the others. He followed them, touching the edge of that shadow—a boundary. A very subtle tremor came from the boundary, like hearing a sound from the shore through a thick layer of water.

That's "outside." The world of the living.

He approached that boundary, sensing through that veil. Someone was speaking, the voice indistinct, like bubbles rising from the depths of water. Someone was crying. Someone was laughing. Someone called a name—whose name was unknown—but as that name passed through the veil, a few points of light suddenly shone for a fleeting moment.

well.

Someone was calling a name by the well. The sound seeped through the boundary, as subtle as a needle piercing His perception. Points of light surged in that direction, clustering on the inner side of the boundary, like a group of children crowding by a window, peering out.

He wanted to go there too. He wanted to be called out to.

But the sound quickly vanished. Silence fell over the well, and the points of light scattered again, resuming their slow, aimless drift. He stood before that boundary, not knowing how long he had stood there—time meant no place here, but He had learned to measure the concept of "long" using the memories within those fragments. A very, very long time.

who I am?

The question resurfaced. This time it hurt more than before, because He already knew so much, yet still didn't know the answer. None of the definitions of "me" within those points of light—name, identity, kinship, experience—applied to Him. He was an exception, something that shouldn't exist.

Unless, of course, He is all of them.

The instant this thought flashed through his mind, he felt his consciousness begin to expand. Yes, if he was everything, then he was both the one who reaps the harvest and the one who is dying; he was both the infant in his mother's arms and the soldier on the battlefield; he was both the throat that calls out a name by the well and the ear that is called but can no longer answer. He was the sum total of all that had ever lived and had vanished into points of light.

He opened his arms, and a thousand "selves" argued within him simultaneously. Each had its own voice, its own desires, its own pain; they squeezed, tore, and devoured each other, shattering his consciousness into powder finer than specks of light. He tried to piece those fragments back together into a complete "self," but the result was unrecognizable—one eye belonged to a soldier, the other to an infant; one hand harvested wheat, the other gripped a broken sword; his heart beat with the pulse of an infant, and his veins flowed with the blood of an old man.

He burst into laughter. The laughter echoed in the sea of light, crashing against the boundaries, against the points of light, against the endless darkness, and then bounced back, overlapping and layering, becoming increasingly shrill and increasingly unlike the sound a "human" could make.

He's gone mad.

From that day on, He would occasionally press against that boundary, listening to the voices of the living with the ears He had gleaned from those fragments. Some cried, some laughed, some called out a name that would never be answered by the well. He listened, sometimes laughing, sometimes crying, but most of the time He did nothing, simply floating in the center of the sea of light, letting those points of light pass through His so-called "body".

He did not have a name of his own. But then, from some unknown time, two syllables began to appear in the voices that seeped into the underworld—like bubbles rising from the bottom of the water, blurry, yet repetitive.

Hades.
