#  Family Totems and Those Favored by Heaven

Every family possesses a totem. It is said that each family has someone who inherits its destiny, and this totem will appear between their brows—the possessor of this totem has the ability to use family relics, and it is unique. Only descendants with exceptional talent or favored by fate can possess a totem, and not every generation has one. The period when a family loses its totem is called the "vacuum period," because being unable to use sacred relics means losing nearly half of its power. During the vacuum period, a family is highly vulnerable to hostile forces—they will probe, provoke, and gradually erode it until a new totem inheritor is born, or the family is completely wiped out.

The inheritance mechanism of family totem is extremely mysterious. It's not a simple matter of bloodline inheritance—sometimes the eldest son of the direct line fails to awaken it, while a collateral son discovers a mark between his eyebrows one morning. The family's attitude towards this phenomenon is extremely pragmatic: anyone who can awaken the totem, regardless of birth, will be designated as the first heir. The importance of bloodline is weighed against spiritual talent, and the latter always prevails over the former in crucial moments. Large families often have multiple wives and concubines to increase the probability of producing a totem heir. Children without a totem are merely population assets; children with a totem are the hope of the family.

Luo Mingyuan's daughter inherited that totem, and because he married into the family, the child took his surname.

Families continuously expand their spiritual gene pool through intermarriage, apprenticeship, and assimilation. A family without a totem is like a toothless tiger; no matter how large its territory or how much wealth it once possessed, it will be devoured by surrounding forces within a few decades. Therefore, the core competition between families is not about territory or wealth, but about the quantity and quality of totem inheritors. Some families are willing to devote everything to cultivating a spiritually gifted child, because once that child awakens their totem, the entire family can extend its lifespan by hundreds of years.

While there is some overlap between those favored by the heavens and those who inherit the family totem, they are not the same thing. The "favor" of a favored person comes from a celestial relic that automatically recognizes them as its master, unrelated to family bloodline; whereas the "talent" of a family totem inheritor comes from the family's inherited fortune. A favored person may have no family background whatsoever, while a family heir may never receive the favor of a celestial relic in their lifetime. Both are exceptional individuals, but the former represents the choice of fate, while the latter represents the continuation of bloodline.

Some children with exceptionally strong spirituality dream of that well. It is an endless, deep well, its water like a mirror, reflecting only distorted outlines. This dream typically occurs at a crucial moment of spiritual maturation—around the age of twenty-five—and is therefore considered the period of final spiritual maturity. Afterward, spiritual growth slows considerably. Children raised in high-pressure environments experience a relatively later spiritual maturation, which can be seen as nature's compensation to them. Spiritually gifted individuals are nurtured and favored in upper-class families, but in lower-class families, such children often die young due to eccentricities or bad luck. Because the higher the spirituality, the slower the awakening, its outward manifestations can easily be confused with intellectual disability.

If a spiritual practitioner's spiritual cultivation far exceeds their psychic potential, it will attract divine jealousy or the covetousness of evil spirits, ultimately leading to an early death. Alternatively, if they see too far but lack the corresponding power, they will experience excruciating pain daily due to the rampant spread of their psychic energy. Such children are considered "innately intelligent" in the upper classes and receive special protection; in the lower classes, they are seen as "ominous monsters," either abandoned or vanishing silently in the dead of night. Spirituality is a luxury—those who possess it must simultaneously possess the resources and status to protect it; otherwise, spirituality itself becomes a death sentence.
