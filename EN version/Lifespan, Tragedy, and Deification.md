#  Lifespan, Tragedy, and Deification

All living beings have their limits. Mortals live for no more than a hundred years, and even the most powerful beings in the Sovereign Realm only live for a few thousand years. The essence of aging is the turbidity of the spirit, like clear water slowly turning into mud. Cultivators can slow down aging and increase their lifespan by constantly absorbing spiritual energy, but this is not infinite—one day, you will encounter that invisible wall.

The name of that wall is "Lament".

As the Grief descends, spirituality begins its irreversible descent into chaos. Consciousness shatters into its earliest particles; the body may still be alive, but the soul has vanished. For cultivators, this is more terrifying than death—death at least has a destination, while the Grief is utter annihilation. Some powerful individuals choose to pass away before the Grief arrives, scattering their ashes into the starry sea, at least preserving a dignified end.

Only the gods possessed limitless lifespans. But the gods perished in Ragnarok, leaving only scattered fragments of their authority in the world. Those beings yearning to transcend these limits have only two paths to follow:

First, there's the deification. People gamble everything to touch that elusive divine authority. Success brings the hope of godhood, failure brings annihilation.

Secondly, acceptance. To peacefully pass away at the end of a thousand years, completing the cycle that life should have.

The vast majority of people choose the latter, because the success rate of deification is negligible. But there are always some unwilling souls who knowingly do the impossible. They are both fools and heroes.

The Underworld is also known as the Sea of Stars—as the name suggests, it lies in the heavens. The stars are the twinkling of slumbering souls. Legend has it that a powerful being once flew tirelessly upwards for a long time, first passing through the "Human Realm"—where spiritual energy still lingers, and floating islands and flying magical beasts occasionally dwell; then ascending into the "Void Realm," where all around is deathly silent, even the wind has ceased to move; continuing to climb, one reaches the "Recognition Layer"—and a strange thing happens: despite flying upwards, one finds oneself falling towards the ground, as if the sky were a mirror, and one is being pulled down by one's own reflection. That powerful being eventually returned to the mountain peak from which he began, his robes fluttering, his face deathly pale. From then on, no one attempted to fly into the Sea of Stars again.

Deep in the ocean lies a colossal rift, said to be the scar left by the fingernail of a fallen god during Ragnarok. The abyss beneath this rift is a forbidden zone for all living things, teeming with monsters: sea serpents with a thousand legs like centipedes, octopuses with human faces, and transparent whales that swallow entire fleets and slowly digest them in the depths. The ocean thus becomes a natural barrier, and fishermen dare only cast their nets near the shore, chanting ancestral incantations. But some desperate individuals always venture into the rift in their small boats—it is said that some have seen light deep within, not the phosphorescence of monsters, but a pure, rhythmic radiance, like a heartbeat.

Psychic energy is the primordial molecule that constitutes matter. This statement needs to be pondered repeatedly—it means that everything, including life itself, is an expression of psychic energy. Understanding this means understanding the deepest laws of this world.
