#  Ending and Cycle

There is no perfect ending in this world.

Villains can never be completely eradicated, evil spirits can never be completely eliminated, social classes will never disappear, and suffering will never cease. Old city-states are breached by evil spirits, new city-states are built on the ruins, and then decades or centuries later, they too will be breached. Everything everyone does is essentially delaying an inevitable end. It's like Sisyphus pushing the boulder, only with more force, more focus, and more ritualistic effort.

But within the core mechanisms of this world lies a tiny exception—the embodiment of the benevolent will of Heaven. Those last vestiges of benevolence that separated before becoming Heaven manifested as spirits and were born into the human world. They possess the immortality of the soul: they do not perish, and can be reincarnated infinitely, retaining their memories. In each life, they might be an ordinary child, an inconspicuous soldier, or a traveler lost in thought on the street. They themselves do not know who they are, but when the world truly needs a ray of light, they will remember—remember why they came into this world.

These people won't save the world. They'll simply make something more memorable in the process of its destruction. What they do might be small: protecting a child in war, caring for a stranger during a plague, offering a word of comfort in despair. These deeds won't change the course of history, end the threat of evil, or eliminate class divisions. But they will leave a mark on some people's memories —when a child grows up, he will remember being saved by a passing Faceless One, and he too will become someone who reaches out to others in need. That is legacy.

This article is dedicated to all who have passed away. Those who are no longer here, those who are still alive but whose edges have been worn down by life, those who struggle alone in the dead of night yet still do not give up. They may never be remembered by history, but their very existence is the reason why civilization is worth continuing.

Don't cry, don't cry, Heath. The yearning to transcend all may eventually vanish in the wind, but before it fades, it has illuminated some. If this world will eventually turn to twilight, then light a lamp in the twilight. The lamp is not meant to illuminate the whole world, but to let people know that even in the deepest darkness, there are still those willing to be the ones holding the torch.

She can finally rest. Every minute and every second of the past felt like tens of thousands of years of torment. But she made it through, and she's still moving forward. There may not be light ahead, but she herself is that light.

If there are no works in this world that truly move me, I will create such an epic myself. First and foremost, it must touch my own heart. I hope my words can offer warmth and solace to those who walk in darkness. May those platinum-like hearts never wither or fade.

I am also a storyteller. When I tell stories, I feel a sense of accomplishment and unprecedented relaxation, as if I don't need to worry about the world's day and night, its noise and clamor, its struggles and survival—I only need to use stories to convey what my soul wants to say, bringing people warmth, luck, and hope. Although I am an ordinary person, and I carry my own burdens, I want to leave something behind. Humans must burn brightly at least once; they cannot gradually become numb and lose their spirituality in the endless trivialities of life, or even lose sight of who they are.

I hope this world has less conflict, less estrangement, less killing, and less indifference. I hope those who read my story can return to their true selves, remembering that a person is first and foremost a life, not an identity, a position, a job, or a study. You don't need to be part of any grand narrative; you are precious enough from birth. Everything you bear is not a sin; your existence is meaning itself.

All that has passed is but a prologue. Every ending is a new beginning, and every extinguished fire has been rekindled by another. As long as there are those willing to hold up a lamp in the darkness, the world is not truly dead.
