# Academy

The Supreme Academy, meant to train spiritual practitioners, should be a sacred place for cultivating elites. Why then does it harbor undercurrents and a dangerous atmosphere? While dangerous, groundbreaking ideas are certainly not good, society is prevented from stagnating precisely because of these idealistic individuals. Restless intellectuals may seem to shake the system in the short term, but in reality, they are using short-term pain to avoid long-term suffering, performing a delicate surgery on the system. So why are the same books readable within the academy, yet banned once they leave?

What would happen if a world-class scholar were caught red-handed in a forbidden area?

The Academy never forbids you from reading, but it certainly doesn't encourage it. You can know, but you can't speak of it. The Academy hopes you become a highly accomplished spiritual practitioner, yet it subtly discourages you from reaching too high a level. Yes, there are two kinds of levels—external spiritual cultivation and internal spiritual cultivation. But what does the Academy truly assess? It's the external level you demonstrate when you directly participate in the Awakening Ceremony after you turn eighteen. Nothing more.

What's even more chilling is that, in order to enhance her own value, she was even sent to an academy to engage in spiritual practice. Surprisingly, her talent for spiritual practice turned out to be quite remarkable. Soon, a powerful figure took notice of her. She didn't resist to the death, because she had already tried countless times—in this kind of world, even with talent, without a background, one can hardly move an inch. Talent needs time to realize its potential, and when weak, without the protection of wings, it often withers before it can even bloom.

The academy's rules are extremely strict. This is a place for cultivating the elite of the divine realm, with exceptionally strict security management, even directly under the watchful eyes of the gods. Under the gods' watchful eyes, the rules cannot be violated. Precisely because of this, almost no one dares to wantonly bully or humiliate their classmates.

The divine kingdom advocates elite education, believing that it is enough for only a few elites to wield great power.

The people in the academy are indifferent. They are causal and do not concern themselves with cause and effect, so they are as hostile as snakes to anything superfluous. They must sincerely pray to the gods every day to obtain divine protection. Although the new generation of scholars have long felt that those formalities are too outdated, no unnecessary complications can be made under the tutelage of the masters.

The professors at the academy possess achievements that most people envy; they are also social butterflies, frequenting various parties and high-society occasions. Their status comes from their profession, and the hierarchy of this profession derives from recognition. Therefore, they are also highly productive, producing theories such as doll theory, and some are simply pure theologians professing their theological theories. In a sense, those who enhance their spiritual power through profession rather than practice are called simplified versions of deities, although no one dares to say it that way. But isn't this path the power of faith?
Similarly, many students in the academy follow this path, while a very small number who cultivate themselves are called outcasts. However, the divine kingdom unusually does not treat them separately because the attention of the masses is a limited resource, and the gods do not want more people to believe in others.
