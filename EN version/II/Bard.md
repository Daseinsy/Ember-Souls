#  Bard

That fallen scion of a noble family eventually became a wandering bard, traveling the continent to tell tales of devastation. His poems were beautiful and moving, bringing hope to the people. His boundless spirit even transcended isolation, forging a new profession.

The protagonist goes to other regions to learn new skills and returns as a weathered middle-aged man, but he still hasn't forgotten the girl from back then.

Evil spirits, evil gods and demons, magical beasts, and twisted beings. Evil spirits are transformed humans; evil gods are beings with great power; magical beasts, lacking spirit, rarely become evil spirits but directly transform into magical beasts; and twisted beings are a group of species in the ocean that call themselves new life. True gods are... Ordinary humans, spiritual cultivators, livestock, spiritual relics, and spirit beasts. Nature possesses two opposing forces: spirit and sorrow.

The so-called evil spirits are nothing more than pitiful souls who have experienced trauma and are filled with distrust of the world.

Jia only knew he was an ordinary child. Every time he mustered hope, the world dealt him a heavy blow, plunging him into darkness. He had no noble family background, no gentle and beautiful mother. He was raised by an old man from a young age, who had witnessed him being beaten to death or committing suicide several times. But even though he was certain he had been disintegrated into atoms, he always reappeared intact, and those around him had no recollection of his deaths. He was always lonely. He had no friends because he was always instinctively wary of everyone, like a wounded animal, and found it difficult to trust others. Yet, he longed to make something of himself, but a blurry figure of a girl would always appear in his dreams, a figure that always brought him tenderness and strength.

The verses of bards do not come entirely from their own fantasies. In fact, even the most fantastical fantasies can be traced back to concrete realities, which they piece together like building blocks.

Therefore, the bard faces an entire world, and a bizarre and wondrous one at that, not an empty, rootless tree.

Only this bard himself knew how lonely and desolate he felt when he was alone in the dead of night. This loneliness tormented him, yet he was still willing to ponder, to question, and to tell the world the stories he had witnessed . He had no friends or family; he wandered the world with nowhere to belong. That was why he still sang and performed, for he had only found this way to fill the emptiness in his heart. For him, being a bard was not just a profession, but a destiny. He had to do something, or he would soon die of thirst.
