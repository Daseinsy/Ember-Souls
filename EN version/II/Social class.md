#  Social class

In any region, class stratification is as natural as a mountain range.

The boundary between the upper and lower classes is clear and solid. In cyber cities, the top of a skyscraper is the sky garden of the upper class, while the bottom is a damp pipe city. There is no elevator between the two, only a cordon. In agrarian civilizations, the east bank of a river is a lord's manor with thousands of acres of paddy fields, while the west bank is a muddy field where tenant farmers bend over to plant rice. On the islands of maritime civilizations, the captain and crew live at opposite ends of the island, drinking from the same sea but eating at different tables.

Class is not oppression, it's gravity—you've just landed where you were meant to be.

Some regions have unified into holy empires, establishing centralized rule spanning the entire continent; others are still embroiled in tribal warfare, with a new lord emerging every few years; still others are constantly at war with each other, the chieftains of the Lava Wastes performing war dances around the earth's fire to pray for victory, while the cultivators of the Thousand-Blade Sword Mountain fly across the border on their swords to slay enemy sentries. Peace is a rare luxury, war is the norm.

The existence of slums is ignored by the respectable. These settlements house artificial arrays that constantly drain the residents' psychic energy and feed it into the psionic network of the upper city. Those repeatedly drained of their energy eventually become numb, their eyes clouded, their steps faltering—they suffer from the only acknowledged disease in the world: poverty. It has no incubation period, it doesn't spread through droplets or contact, yet it's more deadly than any plague. The symptoms are gradually clouding eyes, increasingly unsteady gait, until finally, they curl up in a still, forgotten corner. There is no cure; the only relief is mana—the psychic energy flowing through all things. The poor cannot produce mana, like a dying person unable to breathe their last.

The Upper Town is brightly lit at night, with people partying under the glow of psychic lights. The Lower Town, however, is lit only by artificial candles—the evil spirits cannot see artificial light, its only protection for the slum dwellers. Girls from the Lower Town, even if wronged, are expected to be grateful to their benefactors, because "giving you a chance is already a blessing." But in certain situations—like the Upper Town academy—children from different social classes are placed in the same classroom, with seemingly uniform medical examinations and selection criteria. This is precisely the most suffocating arrangement: it gives you the illusion of having a choice, when in reality you are separated from that aristocratic child sitting in the front row by a wall.

The artisans of the Star Federation had long since reached the limits of the laws of physics. They knew of the existence of atoms and the working principles of forbidden weapons, but all of that was permanently sealed in a black box in the polar ice caps, a place even the Speaker himself dared not approach. The Federation's official stance was: "Those were merely the fantasies of the ancients." But in private, the Federation's top scholars were still secretly researching—not how to manufacture them, but how to make the world never need them again.

In this world without guns and cannons, the air is unusually fresh. Every breath feels like drinking from a mountain spring. The campfire at night is the only source of warmth, and windmills and waterwheels creak and turn, driving stone mills and looms. There is no roar of machinery, no acrid exhaust fumes, only the crackling of firewood and the deep calls of totem beasts in the distance. This is a world stripped of industrial civilization by the laws of physics, yet it retains a certain primitive poetry in the process. Some once dreamed of "conversing with the energy of steel," and those dreamers were called "Iron Summoners"—they believed that deep within the cold metal lay sleeping souls, which could be awakened with the right words. Now, this is a lost song, used by children to fall asleep, dreaming that their shadows transform into armored giants.
