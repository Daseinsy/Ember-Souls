#  A Chaotic World

Purpose: To explain the chaos in the world.

Of course, there are stars in the sky of this world, but they are not stars; they are reflections of this continent. The sky is not really a sky; it is merely a mirror.

What lies in the sky? No one knows. All exploration seems to return to its starting point. Whether you dig downwards or fly upwards, the final result is always the same: you return to where you began.

Of course, you can break through dimensions to witness the reality of chaos, but that is something humans cannot understand, and those who have seen it have gone mad.

You can fly sideways, of course, but the directions in the wilderness are all chaotic. If you don't follow the path of the sages, you may never find the right way.

Even the space here is distorted; one step and you might inexplicably find yourself transported to another place. There are no boundaries. Different city-states might be within reach of each other, or they might be separated by countless light-years.

Even time is distorted, because absolute time, which is the time that has already changed, cannot be reversed, so there is no phenomenon of returning to the future. However, the speed of time that people perceive may be different. You may have just gone out into the wilderness, and your appearance may still be young, but your family members may have all grown old and died.

We are not like that, but we are always unclear about who we are, what we want to do, and what to do after we have done it.

He was in great pain, and in his daze, he didn't even know where he was. Everything around him began to become distorted and unfamiliar, as if there were figures coming and going, but everything was shrouded in fog.

The spirit decays, the spirit rots, the spirit is devoured.

There is no sun in the sky; endless night reigns amidst the ruins. Legend has it that the world once had a sun, but where has it gone?
When he woke up, his mouth tasted of sand.

The rough, rusty sand was mixed with a faint smell of sulfur. He lay face down, cheek pressed against the ground, unconscious for an unknown amount of time. His consciousness was like shattered shards of pottery, barely pieced back together, every crack letting in a draft. He tried to prop himself up, but his arms gave way, and he fell back down. The sensation of his palm brushing against the ground made him pause for a moment. It wasn't soil, nor rock, but something in between—dry, cold, like burnt ashes.

He didn't know where he was.

The thought was like a cold needle, piercing straight into the back of my neck.

He remembered leaving the city, remembered walking along the desolate road, remembered the wind. But what happened after that? He turned over, lying on his back, his chest heaving. The sky was right before him. It was a color he had never seen before. There were no clouds, no stars, only a dim, grayish-blue dome overhead. It was as if someone had hung a stagnant pool of water upside down in the sky, the surface still rippling slightly, the ripples so fine they seemed to breathe. He stared at those ripples for a long time, and found that he could see blurry outlines through them. Mountains, wastelands, broken towers, dried-up riverbeds. These outlines were upside down above him, like another world hanging above.

He suddenly understood.

That is the reflection of this continent.

“The sky is just a mirror.” He heard his own voice, hoarse as if someone else were speaking. He sat up and looked around. Ruins. He was in ruins. All around him were broken walls and rubble, their surfaces covered with thick black ash, as if a great fire had burned for centuries and was still smoldering. There was no firelight. Only eternal night. Eternal night was not absolute darkness; it was closer to a murky void, somewhere between twilight and a dream. Everything in his vision was veiled in a thin, grayish-blue gauze, without shadows.

He looked down at his hands. His palms were still there. His fingers moved freely. But he noticed dark red blood seeping from under the nail of one finger. He couldn't remember when he'd been injured. Maybe just now. Maybe a long time ago.

A gust of wind blew from the depths of the ruins. It whistled through the cracks in the broken walls, like something inhaling deeply. A soft thud came from afar, like a stone being kicked. He turned sharply. A silhouette flickered in the distance, blurry and indistinct, like a human figure. But as he focused his gaze, the silhouette transformed into half a chimney, leaning precariously in the ruins.

He stood up. His knees cracked unsettlingly. He tried to take a step forward. The ground beneath his feet seemed unchanged. But after taking the second step, he realized he was much closer to the low wall at the edge of the ruins. He glanced back; the spot where he had been lying was now far away, like across an entire street. He stopped, his heart pounding in his chest.

The space was wrong. The space here would swallow you whole and then spit you out. A single step could shorten a thousand miles, or it could trap you in a perpetual cycle. He'd heard it before. The old men of the city-states, telling stories by the fireside, always said that beyond the wilderness, borders meant nothing. Different city-states could be reached in an instant, or they could be separated by countless light-years. Maps were useless here; only the Path of the Sage could provide a definite direction. But where was the path beneath his feet? He looked down and saw countless cracks spreading across the surface, like a torn star map. Traces of the path remained, broken into fragments, as if jacked up from the ground and then thrown back down. The runes had all extinguished, leaving not even a trace of warmth.

He felt a chill run down his spine. It wasn't the wind, but something approaching. The chill crept up his spine, inch by inch, like a damp, cold hand. He turned around, but all he saw were ruins . Nothing. Yet the chill on his skin grew stronger. A faint, fishy smell began to fill the air, like seawater, but stronger, more putrid, carrying the sweet, rotten odor of some drowned plant fermenting.

He remembered. To the southeast lay a deep-sea rift. It stretched from the seabed to the continental shelf, and was said to spew forth demons. He had once gazed in that direction from the city-state's watchtower; the sea was black, like a piece of unbreakable jade. But now he could smell the sea. This meant the rift was near, or worse, he was currently on its very edge.

He didn't linger, heading away from the stench. With each step, his nerves were taut, watching his footing. Once, as he took a step, he felt as if a layer of the ground had been ripped away, causing him to stumble. His vision was momentarily stretched into thin lines before snapping back to normal. When he regained his footing, he found himself in a narrow alley, the high walls on either side almost touching his shoulders. The walls were askew, riddled with small, round holes, like countless empty eyes. He instinctively looked up; the sky was still that mirror. There was no reflection of him in the mirror.

How long have you been standing here?

A voice came from behind.

He whirled around. A man stood at the alley entrance, draped in a dark cloak, the hood pulled low, revealing only his chin. His chin was pointed, his skin pale, and his lips pressed into a thin, almost bloodless line. The man leaned against the wall, not approaching, and continued, as if talking to himself, “Don’t be afraid. I’ve been standing here for a long time. You appeared suddenly, like you squeezed out of the wall.”

"Who are you?" he asked.

“Just like you,” the man said, “a lost person.”

Where is this place?

“I don’t know.” The man turned his head, seemingly looking at him, or perhaps listening to something else, his finger lightly tapping the round hole in the wall. “I arrived here three days ago. But in those three days I walked at least forty miles, and the end was still this alley. The walls on both sides kept changing. The number of holes varied. Sometimes there was one more, sometimes one less. But the alley never had an exit.”

"Give it a try," the man said, a strange smile playing on his lips.

He hesitated for a moment, then reached out and pressed his hand against the wall. The wall was cold and rough; his fingers could feel the sharp bristles along the edges of the small holes. He pushed himself up, bracing his feet against the wall, and peered out, trying to see what was behind it. But the instant his gaze passed over the top of the wall, he saw his own back. That back was standing behind him, facing away, at the other end of the alley. His breath caught in his throat, his hand slipped, and he fell. When he looked up again, everything was normal. Behind the wall was still a wall, and above him was still the mirror-like sky.

"Did you see it?" the man asked.

He didn't answer, he just gasped for breath, cold sweat trickling down his forehead.

“What you see is yourself,” the man said. “Everything here will make you see yourself. The outside of the wall is not you; the outside of the wall is the truth you think you should see. But the truth is that you are trapped here. The sky is a mirror, and the earth is the opposite of the mirror. No matter which direction you go, you are just going around in circles between the two mirrors.”

"Then why are you still here?" he asked.

“Because I can’t remember who I am,” the man said. His voice trailed off, carrying a weariness that was almost like a dream. “I remember coming from the north, crossing a wilderness that alters the flow of time, to find something. But now that I’m here, I’m suddenly unsure what I’m looking for. I can’t even remember my own name. My face has probably changed, like those round holes, sometimes a piece appears, sometimes a piece disappears.”

He stared at the man. The body beneath the cloak didn't seem perfectly symmetrical; the left shoulder was higher than the right, as if bones were slowly moving beneath the clothing.

“Spiritual decay.” He said the word, his throat feeling as if it had been burned.

“Perhaps,” the man said, “Perhaps not. Some say it’s the spirit that’s decaying, others say it’s time that’s eating us. Here, time isn’t a line. I saw my childhood self, just now, walking across the wall.”

“We need to find the way through the wilderness,” he whispered, whether to the man or to himself, it was unclear.

“The road?” The man laughed, a short, dry laugh, like a withered branch snapping in the wind. “Didn’t you notice? You’ve been standing on the road for a long time. The road is ruined. This is part of the road. The road paved with the bodies of the sages was turned upside down by something, like a dead snake, belly up. We are now stepping on its bones.”

He looked down. The ground was still grayish-white, crisscrossed with cracks, but those cracks now looked remarkably like broken vertebrae, one by one, extending into the darkness. His legs went weak, as if something deep within him had convulsed. It wasn't an earthquake, but some subtler tremor, closer to the depths of his soul. Like a thousand voices simultaneously sighing, then simultaneously turning over in a dream.

“I want to leave,” he said.

“Let’s go,” the man said. “Go anywhere. It doesn’t matter if you can’t get out. Anyway, there’s no sun in the sky, and the ruins have no end. When you reach the end, you’ll only find that you’re still on this road. And you won’t remember why you started.”

He closed his eyes. In a daze, everything around him became blurry and distorted, as if figures were coming and going, but each one was shrouded in mist, their faces unrecognizable, their voices inaudible. He didn't know where he was, nor what he was supposed to do. He only felt an immense weariness rising from the ground, like vines binding his legs, his chest, and his breath. The weariness was as light as a blanket, yet as heavy as a ruin. He opened his mouth, wanting to shout, but the sound was swallowed by silence, as if it had never existed.

From a distant, extremely far place, a long, resonant sound came, like a bell being struck deep underground. The lingering echo slid across the surface of the ruins, shaking off a few specks of black dust.

He opened his eyes.

He remained standing there. The person was gone. At the end of the alley, a faint, almost white, blue light appeared, like a star about to sink to the bottom of the water.

He took a step towards the light. A soft "crack" sounded beneath his feet, as if something extremely thin and cold had broken. He looked down and saw a black, sticky trail extending from the base of the wall, eventually disappearing into the shadows of the ruins. It wasn't a shadow.

He kept walking. The light flickered, sometimes near, sometimes far, as if guiding him, yet also as if fleeing him. He didn't look back. Because he knew that if he did, he would only see himself.
