#  Mainland China and Transportation

The world that humanity actually inhabits is called the "Phenomenon Realm." The Phenomenon Realm is vast and boundless, divided into seven regions, each with its own unique features, laws, and forms of civilization. Normally, the seven regions exist in different dimensions, isolated from each other—if you stand on the edge of a continent and look out, you see only endless void and mist, unable to see anything on the other side.

However, every seventy years, a celestial phenomenon known as the "Night of Convergence" occurs. At that time, the seven stars align, the dimensional veil between the seven continents thins, and travelers can see the outlines of mountains on the other side, as if they could reach out and touch them. But this is only a visual connection—to truly traverse the void, special means of transportation are still required.

Transportation relied on totems. In city squares stood enormous stone sculptures in the shape of beasts; travelers would place their hands on their bases and silently recite their destination, at which point the totems would awaken. Some would transform into wind birds soaring through the sky, others into earth dragons burrowing into the soil, and still others would unfold like floating jellyfish, gently swaying their tentacles in the rivers. The more mana injected, the more refined the totem's transformation—the totems of nobles could become six-winged golden rocs, while those in the slums could only barely transform into three-legged beasts, needing to rest after every three steps. No one could explain why the totems responded to human will. Craftsmen said the patterns contained ancient runes, priests said the stones came from bones from before the world's creation, while skeptics privately discussed: perhaps the totems never moved at all, but only our perception of space shifted.

Only two kinds of beings are capable of traversing the vast void of stars.

One type is the Celestial Ship, an ancient relic, its hull made of breathing jade. No one knows who built the Celestial Ship, nor what principle governs its navigation; all that is known is that they have existed for a longer period than the combined histories of all civilizations. Celestial Ships are extremely rare, each considered a national treasure or divine object, held by the most powerful forces.

Another type is the Boundary-Breaking Spaceship, manufactured by the Star Federation. The Star Federation is the most technologically advanced continent; its artisans and scholars have long understood the full scope of the laws of physics, even reaching the limits of theory. Forbidden atomic weapons are sealed in black boxes beneath the polar ice caps, and even the Federation's Speaker dares not approach them. The Boundary-Breaking Spaceship is the culmination of the Federation's technology—it does not rely on totems or gods, but rather traverses the void using pure engineering and psionic engines. However, in the general understanding across the seven continents, the spaceship is still considered a "heretical creation," and only residents of the Star Federation would ride it with peace of mind.

The seven continents are normally isolated from each other, resulting in information isolation and vastly different cultures. Under the same sky, some live among the glowing pipes of cyber cities, some walk barefoot in the volcanic ash rice paddies of lava wastelands, and some carve their tribal emblems in snow caves on polar ice fields. They are unaware of each other's existence, or have only heard of "people on the other side of the sea" in legends.

The night of the conjunction of the seven stars is one of the few windows of opportunity. Neutral ports are brightly lit on this night, and travelers from different continents briefly meet, scrutinizing each other's creations, clothing, and languages as if looking into a distorted mirror. They exchange goods, information, and skills, and then each goes their separate ways, never to meet again for another seventy years.

Seven continents, seven distinct civilizations.

Wild tribal civilizations dwell atop the polar ice fields. Wrapped in animal skins, with tribal emblems etched on their teeth, they live by hunting snow beasts. They have no written language, but possess epic tales passed down orally; they have no cities, but altars guarded for generations. The tribal chief is the bravest warrior, and when they dance a war dance around a bonfire, the frozen earth beneath their feet resonates with a deep, dull echo.

The agricultural civilization of the Lava Wasteland has carved miraculous terraced fields from volcanic ash. They irrigate their fields with geothermal heat, and the rice stalks grow taller than a person, imbued with rich spiritual energy. The people here are not skilled in combat, but they are masters of soul forging—refining soul iron from the earth's fire to create agricultural tools, utensils, and weapons. The soul forgers of the Lava Wasteland are renowned in the weapon markets of the Seven Continents.

Cyber civilization is permeated with glowing psionic conduits, cities suspended like a giant net above canyons. Residents exchange information using psionic terminals, and the psionic network permeates every corner of life—transportation, healthcare, education, entertainment. The upper class lives in sky gardens atop skyscrapers, while below lies a damp, conduit-filled city, where the hum of machinery never ceases. Cyberkin are accustomed to implanting psionic circuits under their skin, with a crystal between their brows resembling a third eye.

The manor civilization is picturesque, with carriages moving slowly under the shade of oak trees, and the hems of noblewomen's skirts brushing against the dew-kissed grass. Here, hierarchy is strict yet outwardly respectable; all conflicts are concealed beneath a veneer of etiquette and law. Cultural administrators, quill pens in hand, record the trajectory of every gust of wind on parchment. The manor civilization produces the most scholars, poets, and professionals—because its inhabitants have ample time and leisure to contemplate the meaning of "profession."

A maritime civilization permeated the islands of the endless sea, ships navigating by star charts. Sailors believed in ancient gods slumbering beneath the surface, daring not to disturb them, only singing low, somber songs on deck. Cyborgs removed wooden legs, revealing brass gear joints, and stomped precisely on the deck. This maritime civilization excelled at exploration and surveying, but they were halted by the deep-sea rift—a nightmare for all sailors.

Thousand-Ren Sword Mountain is a mountain range that pierces the clouds, where cultivators fly on their swords to slay spies from neighboring territories. Here, pure sword cultivation is practiced, with the belief that "one sword can break all laws." The domain lord is the one with the highest level of swordsmanship, and each generation of domain lord leaves their sword mark on the summit of Thousand-Ren Peak for posterity to observe.

The cultivation world is composed of numerous sects, such as the Dan Ding Sect, the Lian Qi Sect, and the Ji Mie Sect, each with its own lineage. This is a cultivation civilization in the traditional sense, emphasizing cultivation techniques, elixirs, and Daoist traditions. The cultivation world and Thousand Blade Sword Mountain are constantly at war with each other, yet they briefly join forces during times of invasion by evil spirits—they call this relationship "Daoist Struggle."

Each realm has its own lord. Power structures vary greatly: some lords are wise men who abdicate in favor of their rulers, some are hereditary tyrants, and some have councils elected by all living beings within their realm. But regardless of the system, the lord himself is always the strongest—this is the only common ironclad law across the seven continents.

The priests of the Eternal God Cult kneel in prayer atop a thousand stone steps; the fairies of the Fairy Tale Forest use mana to coax flowers that never fade; in the Realm of Demons, beast tamers, bound by symbiotic pacts with magical beasts, converse with wild creatures day and night. Across the seven continents, there is no unified faith, only self-serving acts of protection and expansion.
