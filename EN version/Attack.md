#  Attack

one

A red alarm blared, light bulbs shattered, and shards of glass littered the desks. Shadows materialized in the air, and a rusty, metallic stench pressed down on everyone's chest. A scream echoed from the end of the corridor—a ripping, short scream, abruptly cut off by something choking it. Then came a second, a third, accompanied by the cracking of concrete, the smashing of desks and chairs, and a damp, sticky slapping sound, one after another, approaching with terrifying dread.

Lin Wang sprang up, his knee slamming into the table. The monster emerged from around the corner of the corridor, its head human and body a snake, its eye sockets filled with two flickering, eerie green flames. With a sweep of its tail, three students were sent flying, crashing into the wall, their blood spraying in mid-air.

Class teacher Zhou stood at the forefront, his spiritual energy forming a barrier. With a flick of his snake tail, the barrier cracked, revealing spiderweb-like patterns. Blood trickled from the corner of his mouth, his feet firmly planted on the ground, his hands trembling as he repaired the defense.

Students scattered and fled. Chen Xiaoyu was grabbed by the hand by Li Meng and squeezed through the stairwell. Li Meng's face was pale, her lips trembled, and tears mixed with sweat rolled down her cheeks. Someone fell—a thin boy's backpack strap got tangled around his ankle, and he lay on the ground shouting "Help!" The crowd surged past until an older female student suddenly turned back and threw him forward.

Scattered flashes of psionic light appeared; some erected shields, some accelerated, and most simply ran instinctively. A girl, exhausted, collapsed to her knees, watching the green flames approach, and closed her eyes in despair.

boom--!

A bolt of blue lightning shot down from the rooftop, striking the monster. The fire giant descended from the sky, slamming its fists into the ground, flames rising three meters high. A giant vine-like serpent burst from the earth, coiling around the monster's tail. The three lines of defense tightened, and the monster finally slowed down.

The remaining students stopped in their tracks. One of them pulled out his phone, his hand trembling as he pointed it at the monster, a hysterical laugh escaping his lips. Several boys patted each other on the shoulder, their eyes sparkling with the excitement of watching a spectacle.

"What are you staring at?!" The special forces captain descended from the sky, the lightning still crackling. "Want to go to the underworld and keep watching?!"

The laughter abruptly ceased. The students stumbled and continued running towards the shelter. Behind them, the monster crashed to the ground, dragged into the sealing chamber, and the green flames went out.

On the day of the memorial service, the sky was overcast. The surviving students stood on the playground, the figures covered in white sheets on the stage a horrifying sight; some bit their lower lips, others had unfocused pupils. It wasn't until Li Meng saw her mother stumble towards her, losing a shoe in the process, that things began to unfold. She knelt on the ground, her mouth agape, unable to utter a sound, tears falling onto the concrete. Her mother hugged her tightly, her arms so tight she could barely breathe, and she finally burst into tears, as if vomiting out all her fear.

Chen Xiaoyu saw her father rushing over, his psionic tail flames still burning, his tie askew. She rushed into his chest, her shoulders heaving, her knuckles white as she clutched his collar. Her father placed his hand on the back of her head, his eyes red as if about to bleed.

The cries blended together, and in the dim light of the sky, trembling figures huddled together.

two

Three days after the memorial service, the school has resumed a semblance of order.

Lin Mo stood under the old sycamore tree in the east corner of the playground, looking at the magic circle on the ground, which was half-hidden by weeds—six circles in a row, with an inverted triangle in the center.

That day, the monster swept through the corridor, and everyone thought it had "just happened" to avoid the load-bearing walls, resulting in far fewer casualties than expected from an evil spirit invasion. The special forces arrived too "just in time," and the monster was sealed away too "smoothly."

Lin Mo remained hidden at the edge of the crowd, his fingertips gleaming with bluish-gray psionic energy. As the monster lunged at the crying girl for the third time, he flicked his wrist—a Weakening spell pierced the monster's tailbone like a fine needle, dimming the cold light of its claws. A fire extinguisher in the corridor "coincidentally" rolled to the monster's feet, causing it to slip, and a second-floor chandelier "coincidentally" exploded above its head. The psionic barrier behind the special forces captain, which had shielded him from the attack, was actually erected by Lin Mo from a distance.

Standing before the magic circle, he realized that the monster's invasion route, the defensive nodes, and the arrival of the special forces team—all of these had been calculated.

The magic circle's patterns pointed to the old altar beneath the school. Someone was using this attack to cover up a deeper ritual—the students who died under the monster's claws had another name in the language of the magic circle: sacrifices.

Lin Mo stood up, brushed off the grass clippings, and turned to walk towards the teaching building. He had only been here for less than two weeks.

three

When Lin Mo was at his most dejected, his neighbor gave him a hot meal.

He moved into a basement apartment with a monthly rent of 800 yuan. His suitcase contained only two sets of clothes and an archaeological shovel. On his first night, he sat on the stairs eating a cold steamed bun when the door next door opened. A woman in an apron poked her head out, paused for a moment, and then smiled: "Hungry? Come in."

Her name is Su Wan, she's twenty-nine years old, and works at the bookstore at the street corner. She'd bring over hot soup when she stayed up late, saying through the crack in the door, "Don't stay up too late." When she smiled, there were fine lines at the corners of her eyes. The scallions in the soup were finely chopped, floating on the oil like tiny pieces of spring.

Then she died. She was hit by an out-of-control truck on her way home from get off work and died at the scene. The traffic police ruled it an accident, the driver was detained for drunk driving, and the case was closed.

Lin Mo attended the memorial service. Standing in the last row, she looked at her photo hanging among the white flowers.

But his intuition told him something was wrong—at the time the truck hit her, he was reading in his room when a sudden chill ran down the back of his neck. He rushed to the window, but couldn't see anything in the darkness.

He went to the scene afterward. The skid marks were straight, with no signs of steering. The drunk driver said he "didn't see anyone," but there were streetlights on that section of the road, and Su Wan was wearing a white coat. Someone had used psychic interference on her as she crossed the road—not to "kill," but to "make her unable to see the car."

He crouched where she had fallen, his palms flat on the ground. There was a very faint trace of psionic energy in the cracks of the asphalt—the same kind as the aura on the magic circle in the school playground.

He died before his memorial service. And the murderer may be at the source of the school's magic circle.

Four

The archaeology expedition team has set off. Lin Mo is a junior, specializing in ancient ruins.

Three months ago, he found a jade pendant in a hidden compartment in a Western Zhou Dynasty site. A month ago, he found a bronze ring in a Warring States period tomb burial pit, its inner ring engraved with three concentric spiral patterns—matching two symbols of a magic array. He never told anyone. Those things were looking for him—he threw the jade pendant into the river, and it appeared under his pillow the next day; he locked the ring in a drawer, and it returned to his finger on the third day.

He sat by the campfire in the camp, leaning against the old locust tree, his knees bent, his chin resting on his arm. The firelight illuminated his profile, casting a long shadow.

Old Zhang squatted down next to him with a bowl of instant noodles, shoving the noodles into his hands: "Are you hungry? Go home and eat."

Lin Mo looked down at the bowl of noodles, the steam rising onto his face. He opened his mouth, but said nothing.

He felt an immense power within him—a psychic energy so abundant it could block monsters from breaking through armor, become invisible, and create "accidents"—but he didn't know what to do with it. He wanted to live, to protect those who gave him hot soup, the neighbors who helped him collect his laundry, and those struggling at the bottom of society like himself. But he didn't know how to protect them.

He lowered his head and took a bite of noodles. It was hot. "...So delicious."

five

On his way back to the city, he took a secluded alley.

Footsteps behind him—three people, their steps practiced. Lin Mo stopped: "...Come out."

The burly man gripped the psionic short stick: "Hand over the psionic device."

He slowly turned around and looked at them for three seconds. "...Okay."

Thirty seconds later, the three men lay sprawled in the alley. The short stick, broken in two, was stuck in a crack in the wall. Lin Mo squatted down, pulled the burly man's money pouch from his waist, and took out an identity token—Lower District · Ke Qing.

At the corner of the alley, a glimpse of clothing flashed by. A girl huddled behind a trash can, hands covering her mouth, eyes wide. He looked at her, raised his right hand, and his fingertip traced a talisman in the air—a thin, bluish light entered her brow. A silencing spell. She wouldn't tell anyone about tonight, she would forget his face.

"Don't walk through alleys like this again." He turned and disappeared into the night.

He didn't kill her.

---

six

The following evening, Lin Mo used his psionic energy to cover his facial features, put on the "Ke Qing" token, and entered the lower level—the shadowy surface of the city's underground. Dim wall lamps hung on rusted pipes, and the air was filled with dust and the sour smell of inferior fuel.

In a private room on the second floor of the Fengya Pavilion teahouse, a holographic mural flowed, and the air was filled with the fragrance of sandalwood. Seven or eight people sat there; some wore robes embroidered with high-level spiritual runes, while others twirled spiritual rings between their fingers. Gold members.

"The auction price of the ruins in the southeast area has gone up again."
"The Holy Maiden of the Holy Church has changed again."
"It wasn't a replacement, it was disappearance. Last month, the saintess turned into a pool of blood on the altar..."

Lin Mo's teacup paused at his lips. Two months ago, during a field expedition, he encountered someone—a woman with a white veil covering her face, her clothes fluttering in the wind, who grabbed him by the back of the collar at the edge of a cliff and threw him to safety. "Be careful," she said. Through the veil, there was an indescribable weariness in her eyes—like someone who had lived for a very, very long time.

"...What's been happening with the Holy Church lately?" he asked in the voice of "Qing Keqing".

The people at the table exchanged glances. "The new Holy Maiden took office last month. But the old Holy Maiden didn't die... Someone saw her sit up in the blood pool and talk to the new Holy Maiden."

Lin Mo gently put down his teacup. "...The Holy Maidens of all generations, were they all the same person?"

The tea table fell silent. Silence was the answer.

---

seven

Three days later, Lin Mo sneaked into the forbidden underground area of the Holy Church.

Using the identity of "Qing" to obtain an outer passage talisman, she stealthily passed through three blocked doors, hugging the wall. The tunnel grew deeper and deeper, and dark red patterns appeared on the walls—exactly the same as the magic array on the playground.

At the end was a pool of blood. Ten meters square, its surface as calm as a dark red mirror, with light slowly flowing at the bottom—like some kind of creature breathing. In the center of the pool sat a woman, dressed in a saint's robe, a white veil half-draped over her shoulders, her long hair cascading into the water, her eyes closed, her lips slightly moving.

She opened her eyes. In that instant, his psychic senses felt as if they had been struck by a blunt knife—blood surged, and a non-human groan echoed throughout the space.

She looked at the shadows where he was hiding. "...You've come."

"Are you wondering why all the Holy Maidens look the same?" She stood up from the blood pool and walked barefoot to the edge. "Because they are all me. Each time I parasitize a body, my spirit returns to the pool and is then infused into the next one. That monster in the school, I released it too. Su Wan, I had someone deal with her too."

When she said "handle," her tone was as flat as if she were talking about the weather.

Lin Mo stepped out of the shadows and deactivated his invisibility. His face was calm, but his fingers were trembling. "...Why?"

“Because the school’s magic array needs a spiritual supply. Monsters are the catalyst, panic is the fuel, and death is the sacrifice. Su Wan—she shouldn’t know so much. Your spirituality is too strong; everyone you touch leaves a trace. She was touched by you, so she must disappear.”

Lin Mo closed his eyes. He remembered that bowl of soup. Scallions floated on the oil.

"...Then why are you telling me all this?"

The saintess smiled. The exact same smile she had when she saved him on the cliff. "Because I want you to join me—to become the next 'me'."

The sound of wind surged from the depths of the tunnel, passing by his ears like an ancient echo. He opened his eyes to look at her, as if looking into a mirror.

"...Half of my spirituality is yours, right?"

The saint did not deny it.

"That jade pendant. That ring. Those 'adventures'. They were all given to me by you. You chose me."

The wind blew again. This time he heard the answer in the wind: "You were always one of my vessels. It's just that you are too strong, and I can't send you in for the time being."

Lin Mo stood rooted to the spot, like a small animal cornered against a wall. Everything he wanted to protect—Su Wan, Lao Zhang, the girl he had let go—was now entangled in this web. He didn't want to become "her," but he didn't know if he could ever escape.

The saintess stood by the pool and reached out her hand to him. "Are you hungry?"

His voice was as gentle as Su Wan's when she asked him through the crack in the door back then.

Lin Mo didn't move. His fingertips were trembling. The wind was so strong that it drowned out his next sentence—

“I heard you.”
