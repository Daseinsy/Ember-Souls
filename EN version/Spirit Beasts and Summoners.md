#  Spirit Beasts and Summoners

The status of summoners

Summoners are symbols of exceptional innate spirituality and among the noblest professions in this world. This isn't because they are the strongest in combat—in fact, a summoner without a spirit beast is even less powerful than a novice swordsman—but because what they possess is irreplaceable: a life partner who will never betray them, and a second life that can be given up at any time. In a world with rigid social hierarchies, summoners rely not on resources or bloodlines, but on their innate and inalienable spiritual affinity. This is why major powers place such importance on spirituality testing—a potential summoner is worth a hundred soldiers.

The cost of life under the contract

Spirit beasts are best raised from infancy; otherwise, the results will be less effective. Adult spirit beasts possess independent spiritual structures, and forcibly forming a contract may lead to backlash. Contracts consume mental energy; forcibly signing one when mental energy is insufficient will result in a fatal brain explosion—this is not an exaggeration, but a literal description of a shattered skull. The core of the contract is the connection of life: the spirit beast bears the fatal damage suffered by the contractor; as long as the spirit beast lives, the contractor will not die. Therefore, the relationship between a summoner and a spirit beast far exceeds that of master and servant; it is more like folding one's soul in half, sharing the weight of the same life.

embers of the fire

The first spirit beast, "Xin," brought an end to the supernatural era—a fact recorded in every textbook. But few know that Xin's death wasn't a natural one—it was "killed." Different factions fought each other for control of Xin, ultimately leading to its exhaustion and death in the chaos. After Xin's death, a temple was built in every settlement, and the sacred fire burning within is said to be Xin's last flame. This sacred fire can dispel evil, but its effectiveness diminishes with distance from the temple. In places too far from the temple, people can only rely on their own lamps—and evil spirits cannot see artificial light; this is the only protection for the slum dwellers.

Spirit beasts can fall. When a spirit beast is corrupted by evil spirits, or its contractor goes astray, the spirit beast will decay into a form called "Sorrow"—an undead soul that plunders spiritual energy to replenish itself. This corruption is irreversible, and the spirit nurturer of such a spirit beast is called an evil spirit master, the primary target of righteous cultivators. Behind every evil spirit master lies a story—about greed, about despair, about the obsession of "I just wanted to protect someone" that ultimately led astray.

Spiritual beings conceal themselves and are favored by Heaven.

Spiritual relics possess a natural characteristic known as "self-concealment." Without a designated owner, they remain in a state of obscurity, indistinguishable from ordinary objects, extremely difficult to discover, and requiring specific conditions to activate. Once, a sacred spiritual relic stood on a bustling street in a grand city, touched, examined, and even kicked by countless people, yet its true identity remained undiscovered for millennia—until a wandering child, on a rainy night, happened to touch the correct spot, awakening this dormant heritage. That child later became the guardian of that city.

Some spiritual relics exist in the starry sky, never falling to earth. They float in the void, like silent stars. But they may automatically choose their master—directly recognizing a master at a suitable juncture of destiny. These extremely rare individuals are called the Chosen Ones. The birth of a Chosen One is unpredictable; they might be an orphan from the slums or the sole heir of a family. Destiny doesn't consider birth, only whether the wavelength of their spirit resonates with that spiritual relic. Legend has it that the reason each spiritual relic chooses its master is extremely private—it might simply be because a person's gaze, a murmur, or even an unspoken wish resonated with the relic's original owner's final obsession.

Spirit beasts originate from the spirit realm where all life consciousness has resided. They are born from belief—when a certain thought, a certain desire, or a certain collective emotion gathers to a sufficient concentration in the spirit realm, a spirit beast will be born. Spirit beasts are not biological beings; they have no flesh and blood, no physiological needs, and no lifespan limitations. They are a personification of spirituality, capable of functioning solely through spiritual energy, never tiring, never hungry, and virtually omnipotent, but they must depend on the spirit of their contractor to exist in the phenomenal world.

Summoners are among the noblest professions in this world. A symbol of exceptional innate spirituality, they can communicate, form contracts, and fight alongside spirit beasts. These spirit beasts can be summoned to protect their masters, carry out missions, and participate in battles. While city-states are never short of skilled psionicists, only a select few can become summoners, as forming contracts requires a spiritual reserve far exceeding that of ordinary people and an innate spiritual affinity. Forcibly forming a contract with a spirit beast whose spirituality is insufficient will result in a fatal brain explosion—the spirit beast's backlash directly affects the spiritual realm, more deadly than any physical injury.

Spirit beasts are best trained from infancy; otherwise, the results will be less effective. Young spirit beasts have not yet fully developed their spirituality, making them more likely to form a close symbiotic relationship with their contractors, with the compatibility naturally increasing over time. Adult spirit beasts, on the other hand, already possess independent will and habits. Forcing a contract often leads to mutual repulsion, or even the spirit beast turning on the contractor and causing death. Therefore, major powers select talented children from a young age for spirit beast training—this is the most expensive form of education, affordable only to a few families or powers.

Spirit beasts can evolve. As the contractor's strength and compatibility increase, the spirit beast can break through its current form and evolve into a higher-level being. The direction of evolution is not entirely random; it is influenced by the contractor's spiritual qualities, the spirit beast's bloodline origins, and the events they both experience together. An ordinary fire lizard spirit beast might evolve into a lava dragon after the contractor undergoes a life-or-death trial related to flames—provided that its bloodline already contains dragon-like factors. Without that root, no amount of opportunity will sprout.

The core principle of the contract is the bond of life. Fatal injuries sustained by the contractor are absorbed by the spirit beast; as long as the spirit beast lives, the contractor will not die. Conversely, injuries sustained by the spirit beast are also shared by the contractor—it's a two-way connection. Therefore, the spirit beast is not merely a combat unit in battle; it is the contractor's second life. This is why summoners often cherish their spirit beasts as much as their own lives—if the spirit beast dies, a piece of the contractor's soul will be forever missing.

However, some have broken this rule. Some have transformed themselves into spirit beasts, abandoning their physical bodies and existing solely as spiritual energy; others have monopolized spirit beast resources, trading them as commodities; still others have spirit beasts that have taken human form and mingled in human society. When a spirit beast possesses complete self-awareness, emotions, and desires, the boundary between it and humans becomes blurred. Those transformed spirit beasts are sometimes more human than humans—they retain the purity of spirit beasts but have learned the complexities of humans.

Even spirit beasts can fall. When a spirit beast's spirit is corrupted by evil spirits, or when its contractor goes astray, the spirit beast will decay into the form of "Sorrow"—an undead soul that plunders spiritual energy to replenish itself. Its spirit nurturers are called evil spirit masters, the primary targets of righteous cultivators. The first spirit beast, "Xin," appeared at the end of the supernatural era. At that time, evil spirits roamed freely, gods fell, and humanity was on the verge of extinction. Xin brought a brief period of peace with its power, ending that darkest age. Every settlement had a temple to Xin, where sacred fire dispelled evil—Xin's final gift. After Xin's death, different gods fought fiercely for Xin's authority, causing the heavens and earth to shatter, and the once unified continent was broken into multiple pieces. Ultimately, the weakened gods were annihilated by evil spirits and demons, their scattered spirits drifting into the void. But Xin's spirit still exists in the sacred fire—as long as the fire remains, the spirit beast will not truly perish.
