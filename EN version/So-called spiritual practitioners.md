#  So-called spiritual practitioners

The sensation of psychic energy flowing through one's fingertips is like touching a warm river. It is not a mysterious power, but rather the primordial molecules that constitute all things—every inch of land, every wisp of wind, every drop of seawater is woven from countless psychic energy particles. People extract, refine, and seal it, thus creating the currency "Mana." When the light points within the crystal shell flicker, some see livelihood, some see cultivation, and some see explosive power capable of tearing the heavens apart. But psychic energy intensity is ultimately just a measure of short-term bursts—a chef at the beginner level might have a psychic energy reading of only seven hundred, yet can use a cleaver to create flavors that bring tears to diners' eyes; while a master with psychic energy exceeding one million, if unfamiliar with life, might burn their fingers while brewing tea. This is how professions are born: the Way of Heaven does not acknowledge useless needs; every profession is a symbol forged from the trials of human life. Swordsmen are born from battle, scholars are born from the pursuit of knowledge, and the profession of "cleaner" is said to have emerged because an ancient city had accumulated too many fallen leaves. An old man swept them every day and actually discovered the rhythm of the wind on the tip of his broom, and the words "cleaner" appeared above his head.

That was the first time young Arno had ever seen a profession manifest. The blacksmith on the street corner was swinging his hammer; amidst the blazing sparks, the pale gold words "Weapon Maker" floated above his head, the characters like flowing fire, lava seemingly surging between the strokes. Every living being could read this script—even a blind person could sense the temperature and weight of the characters with their eyes closed. But profession was a highly private matter; no one would actively display it. It would only surface during battle, like a trump card forced to be revealed. Therefore, the people in this city maintained a polite distance from one another: you didn't know if the old lady selling breakfast was a "Gold-tier" "Witcher," nor did you know if the child squatting by the wall playing with the cat might suddenly have a "Sword Saint (LV.99)" appear above his head. No one would rashly provoke anyone, because once a blade was drawn, the level and profession above the opponent's head could crush you.

Arno was here today for the Heavenly Stone test during his coming-of-age ceremony. The dusty gray stone stood in the center of the square, said to detect psionic energy levels, its mechanism unknown—unlike a thermometer with markings, it simply emitted different colors: gray, red, yellow, dark gold, purple-gold… corresponding to Beginner, Silver, Gold, Epic, Legendary, and Sovereign, respectively. When Arno placed his palm on it, the stone trembled violently, first gray, then red, then leaping to yellow—the onlookers cheered. But the next second, the color abruptly sank into a dark red-black, like boiling magma in an abyss; the stone let out a groan, and fine cracks appeared on its surface. The city lord frowned and whispered, “Red-black…never recorded.” Arno withdrew his hand; his palm was burning hot. He felt a strange power surge through his veins, but that power quickly receded, leaving him bewildered.

He was unaware that he had been marked by the Heavenly Stone. That night, an old man in a gray cloak stopped him in a slum. “Your psychic energy reading is only eight hundred, which is merely the initial stage of cultivation,” the old man said, lifting his hood to reveal a pair of golden eyes. “But your ‘field’—that invisible aura—makes me feel as if I have encountered a sovereign.” The old man handed him a dull ring, saying, “This is a spiritual relic. Legend has it that it is the relic of a certain ‘King’ professional, but it has been obscured for a thousand years, and no one can resonate with it. Try it.” Arno took it; the ring was cold. He closed his eyes, and his fingertips touched a very shallow engraving inside the ring—the engraving suddenly became scorching hot, and an image flashed before his eyes: a king raising this ring on a battlefield at dusk, the psychic energy of thousands of soldiers converging into light, he shouting a lost liberation phrase, the earth cracking open, and the enemy army vanishing into nothingness. Arno opened his eyes; the ring now shone with a warm, dark golden luster. The old man nodded: "You have found its story, and the initial connection has been established. The more you learn about this king's life, the more you will be able to unleash its power—and one day, you may even be able to use the 'Liberation Skill' to mobilize the ultimate spirit of heaven and earth. But after that, it will slumber for a long time."

Arno then understood that spirit relics were not cold weapons, but solidified history and emotions. They were divided into sacred relics and evil relics; the former carried the weight of protection and sacrifice, while the latter were imbued with curses and greed. Each one was shrouded in secrecy; if not destined, it could remain unnoticed even if placed in a bustling city for a thousand years. It is said that there was once a sacred spirit relic—a ruler belonging to a "teacher"—standing on the main road of a prosperous capital, used as an ordinary hitching post, until three thousand years later when a truant child leaned against it and lamented the hardships of his studies. Suddenly, the ruler glowed and recognized him as its master. And above the starry sky, countless unfaded spirits slumbered, waiting for the "chosen one" at the pivotal moment of destiny—a soul one in a million—to be awakened by the light of a falling meteor in the dead of night, the lines of a contract appearing on their fingertips.

Family totems are another form of inheritance. Every ancient family possesses a totem mark, which only appears between the eyebrows of the descendant who inherits the family's destiny. When the totem lights up, the resonance of the family's sacred relic increases significantly , almost unleashing its full power. However, totems are not present in every generation; the period when a totem is lost is called a "vacuum period"—the family is like a family missing an arm. Arno suddenly remembered that he seemed to have a small birthmark on his left eyebrow bone, and since childhood, people had laughed at him, saying he looked like a bird that hadn't opened its eyes. He touched it, and the birthmark was now slightly warm.

The old man put away the ring and said meaningfully, "One can cultivate multiple professions simultaneously, each with a hundred levels. After reaching the maximum level, one advances to the next, but no one knows what the path ahead will lead to—99% of Sword Saints are stuck at the last level, dying without knowing whether 'Sword Saint' is followed by 'Sword God' or 'Sword Demon.' And you, with your red and black celestial stone, your totems appearing and disappearing, and your ability to resonate with spiritual relics… will you be the one to break through the limits?" The old man disappeared into the night, leaving Arno standing in the empty alley, the moonlight cold above him, the distant sound of a sacred choir singing—it is said that the song can weaken the corruption of evil spirits, but at this moment it felt more like a reminder: this world has seven continents, cultist conspiracies, monsters in the abyss, and civilizations like the Star Federation that touch the limits of physics. And all of this ultimately boils down to one judgment: how strong your spiritual energy is, how high your profession is, how ancient your relics are.

But Arno remembered his mother's words: "Psionic energy is color, profession is word, and relics are stories. But people don't live to become numbers or labels." He clenched the Mana coin in his hand—that low-level currency worth only ten units of psionic energy—and suddenly smiled. Even with poverty and disease as their constant companions, even with class as an iron wall, those unpolished initial dreams, those Ironcallers who fantasized about "conversing with steel" on blueprints, those tribal youths who carved their tribal emblems on the icy plains, were all resisting being defined in their own ways.

He decided to go home and first level up his newly awakened "Apprentice Swordsman" class to LV.1 before researching the story of the ring. The road ahead was long, and the day of the Seven Stars' conjunction was still three years away—by then, the passage between the seven continents would open, and perhaps he could board the Transcendent Ship to see the Prophecy Machine of the Star Federation, meet the Flower Bearers of the Manor Civilization, and listen to the breath of demons at the edge of the Abyss. But what he wanted most was to find the old man in the gray cloak and ask him clearly: what exactly was this red and black color?

In the city at night, totem-like vehicles rumbled through the streets, their soft glow emanating from the psionic streetlights. Arno looked up and saw the sea of stars—the place known as the underworld—the stars twinkling gently, as if departed souls were watching over the mortal realm. He suddenly felt that he might also be a punctuation mark in some ancient story, and the entire world's setting was waiting for him to write a new line of text.
