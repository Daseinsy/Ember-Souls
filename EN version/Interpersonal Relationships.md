#  Interpersonal Relationships

All the laws, powers, totems, and evil spirits of this world ultimately point to the same core—the relationship between people. This is the fundamental basis of all things and the starting point of all stories.

The bonds between family members, the competition and support among peers, the rivalry, domination, humiliation, and revenge between the inferior and the superior, the possessiveness and sacrifice in love, the trust and betrayal in friendship, and the struggle, hope, pain, and enjoyment within oneself—these relationships form the entire backdrop of the narrative. Any grand worldview, if stripped of the concrete connections between people, is merely an empty collection of settings. And any conflict, whether it occurs between city-states or on a spiritual level, can ultimately be traced back to a simple fact: there will always be someone who wants to dominate others.

This is the essence of villainy. Not an external enemy, not a demon, not some abstract concept of "evil"—but the living being itself. Demons certainly exist; extremely sinister things like Cthulhu do indeed lurk in the shadows of this land, but understand that they are all born from evil intentions. Evil spirits, evil gods, and abyssal monsters are essentially products projected from the will of the living being itself. So-called demons are merely tools cultivated by the powerful.

Therefore, there is no such thing as a peaceful ending after defeating the final boss. Villains can never be completely eliminated; wherever there is life, there will be conflict and malice, and where there is malice, new evil spirits will spring up. The truly profound conflicts are never between humans and demons, but between humans themselves—lords siding with evil spirits for immortality, neighboring states waging war for expansion, families turning against each other for totem inheritance, and commoners humbly kneeling in worship for survival.

No snowflake is innocent. History is a trend driven by the majority, with great figures merely being thrust into the limelight. This isn't to say that great figures have little influence, but rather that their impact and the role they unconsciously play in history are inextricably linked to the collective consciousness of their era. For example, faith came first, then the gods. The yearning for light came first, then the torch of civilization. The obsession with immortality came first, then the traps of evil gods.

What kind of person one chooses to become is often not determined by grand historical events, but rather by the accumulation of small, specific, and even unintentional daily choices. Some people ultimately accomplish deeds that define an era, some are swept away by the vicissitudes of life, some have their edges smoothed by fate, and some become the very thing they once despised. But amidst all this, there are always those who choose kindness, choose perseverance, and choose to keep a small light burning in the darkness. These people are not necessarily heroes; they have no names and will not appear in any epics, but they constitute the most resilient foundation of this world.

The most endearing thing about the children of humanity is that there will always be those naive adults who believe in the light. They may be small, they may be humble, they may have been hurt countless times by the world, but they still hold onto that tiny flame, refusing to be extinguished. Don't forget, a person is always a life, not a certain identity, a certain position, a certain job, or a certain field of study. The purpose of writing is to let those people feel—that they have already done wonderfully, that they have not given up hope despite so many difficulties, that they have held onto their innocence and kindness even though they have been deeply hurt by the world. They don't need to prove anything to anyone, because their very existence is a miracle of the human spirit.
