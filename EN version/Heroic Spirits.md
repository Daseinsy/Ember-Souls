#  Heroic Spirits

Heroic Spirits are a different kind of existence from Faceless Men. Faceless Men are living people who chose to transcend, while Heroic Spirits are dead people who chose not to leave.

When a city-state, a land, or a civilization faces a massive invasion by evil spirits, and resistance is impossible, sometimes people voluntarily offer sacrifices—transforming their souls into pure willpower and merging them into the earth at the cost of their lives. This sacrifice is not a religious ritual, not a forced act of sacrifice, but a choice made voluntarily by those who have already seen through the inevitable end. They know that the consequence is relinquishing reincarnation, relinquishing peace, relinquishing any form of "afterlife"—the soul will forever cease to belong to itself, becoming a part of the earth, a silent barrier.

After the heroic spirits became dead, they lost consciousness, memories, and the capacity for joy and sorrow. Their willpower flowed beneath the earth like underground water veins, forming an invisible magnetic field. Evil spirits and demons were suppressed by this field, unable to easily expand their territory. This is the fundamental reason why the forbidden zone of life did not continue to expand—not because humanity won the war, but because the sacrificed themselves provided a buffer of delay. Beneath every inch of land that was preserved, at least one heroic spirit lies buried.
On the ruins of war, civilizations are rebuilt time and again. The wealth gained from the wilderness allows civilizations to continue, but each rebuilding is accompanied by new sacrifices. New heroic spirits are born with each day of decay—and when enough heroic spirits accumulate on a piece of land, that land becomes "safe": evil spirits cannot approach , and monsters cannot dwell there. This is why ancient city-states are always more stable than newly built ones, because the soil beneath their feet holds countless silent souls accumulated over millennia.
A few know of the existence of heroic spirits, but most only vaguely sense that "this land has guardian spirits." People explain the immunity to evil spirits as "ancestral protection," and the absence of plagues as "good feng shui." No one is willing to delve into the truth behind it, because the truth is too heavy. Those heroic spirits have given up reincarnation, given up rest, and guard this land in the form of spirits. They don't know if their sacrifice is meaningful; they only know they must do it. Some people and events deserve to be remembered in art. Although history may not remember, some people do.
