#  The Beginning of Spirituality

The central lecture hall is unbelievably large.

Lin Jing sat in the seventh row of the circular seating area, her gaze sweeping over the dense crowd in front of her, landing on the tiny figure on the distant stage. The professor, dressed in a gray robe, stood before a pure white stone wall, like a lone chess piece on a chessboard. Dozens of fluorescent lights hung high from the dome of the lecture hall, illuminating the entire space as if it were daytime; it was said to be able to accommodate 80,000 people simultaneously.

"Today we will talk about the spiritual practice methods and principles of spiritual attainment."

The professor's voice wasn't loud, yet it reached everyone's ears clearly. Lin Jing knew this was the effect of "sound transmission," a very basic spiritual application, but when performed by a legendary spiritual cultivator, it still inspired awe.

The professor raised his hand.

Above the dome, countless tiny points of light fell like rain, each one precisely sinking into the center of a student's forehead. Lin Jing felt a memory suddenly appear in his mind—knowledge of basic spiritual theories, clearly structured, as if he had already learned it. But he also knew that this "spiritual infusion" could only transmit knowledge itself, and could not replace true understanding.

"I know some of you are thinking, if there are such methods, why bother coming to class in person?" The professor lowered his hand, his gaze sweeping across the room. "Because knowledge can be imparted, but understanding cannot be replicated. What I'm about to talk about is the real reason you're here."

Lin Jing involuntarily sat up straight.

"The path of cultivation consists of nothing more than four things: innate spirituality, acquired resources, self-training, and the accumulation of time." The professor held up four fingers. "Talent determines the starting point, resources determine the upper limit, training determines efficiency, and time determines depth. All four are indispensable, but their relative importance and urgency vary from person to person."

A student in the front row raised his hand. The professor nodded.

"Professor, which of these four is the most important?" The student's voice was clear and bright, carrying the sharpness unique to young people.

The professor remained silent for a moment.

“If you’re asking ‘to become a spiritualist,’ then talent is the most important thing. Without spiritual talent, nothing else matters.” He paused. “But if you’re asking ‘to go further on this path,’ then the answer is—both are important, and neither is important.”

What does this mean?

“What I mean is,” the professor’s voice was slightly lower, “that spiritual practice is not a multiple-choice question. People who try to rank them are often just trying to find a reason to convince themselves because they feel powerless in some aspects.”

Lin Jing was slightly startled. These words were like a fine needle, piercing something indescribable. A few low murmurs arose around them, then quickly fell silent.

"Next, we'll talk about realms." The professor turned around and drew a simple circular pattern on the stone wall. "The realm of a spiritual cultivator resides in the sea of consciousness, not in the dantian. This is quite different from the records of some ancient schools of thought."

He looked at the crowd, his gaze sharp as an eagle's.

"What does this mean? It means that your cultivation cannot be abolished. There is no such thing as 'wasting your cultivation.' Once a realm is formed, it becomes intertwined with your soul and becomes a part of your existence."

Lin Jing heard someone in the back row gasp.

"The only way to abolish a spiritual cultivator's cultivation level is to destroy their brain. And to do that, you must have already killed them," the professor said calmly. "In other words, abolishing cultivation is equivalent to killing someone. So those novels that say 'abolish your cultivation but spare your life' are pure nonsense."

A soft laugh rang out in the classroom, but Lin Jing noticed that the professor's expression was not relaxed.

“But that doesn’t mean spiritual cultivators won’t lose their power,” the professor continued. “Let me give you an example from the history of the divine kingdom. Four hundred years ago, the head of the Treva family was captured in a coup. His enemies wanted to strip him of his abilities but dared not kill him, so they could only seal his consciousness with spiritual magic. This patriarch lived for sixty-three years before passing away, during which time his power was completely suppressed, but his realm never disappeared. As long as the soul is immortal, the academy has ways to keep you alive and ways to restore you.”

Lin Jing felt a slight chill run down her spine. Alive, yet powerless for sixty-three years. This example was more powerful than any intimidation.

"So, what is spirituality?" the professor suddenly raised his voice. "Spirituality is the foundation of your spiritual realm, an irreplaceable life imprint. It cannot be obtained through infusion, but can only be enhanced through enlightenment."

He stepped down from the podium and paced back and forth in front of the stone wall.

“There is only one way to train and elevate one’s spirit—through all experiences.” The professor said slowly and deliberately, “Yes, everything. The paths you have walked, the things you have done, the people you have loved, the people you have hated, what you have lost, what you have gained. All of these will be etched into your spirit and become your unique mark.”

Lin Jing felt her heart skip a beat. All her experiences. This statement was too general, yet too accurate.

“That’s why modern spiritual cultivators have transcended the limitations of the physical body.” The professor stopped, his gaze sharp. “High-level spiritual cultivators, especially those who reach the Legendary Realm, can modify their physical bodies, even transforming into giants or behemoths. But—”

He raised one hand, as if to suppress the expectations of the entire audience.

“You rarely see this situation. Because the giant form consumes a lot of energy, no spiritual cultivator would use it lightly. More importantly, maintaining that form will accelerate the fatigue of the sea of consciousness, and long-term use is tantamount to slow suicide.”

Lin Jing suddenly felt an invisible pressure spreading from the direction of the podium.

The professor's body began to change.

The sound of his grey robe ripping open was exceptionally clear in the silent lecture hall. The professor's muscles bulged, his bones thickened, and coarse, dark grey hair sprouted from his skin. His face elongated, his teeth transformed into two curved, enormous tusks, and in a few seconds, he transformed into a mammoth tens of meters tall.

Those eyes still belong to the professor.

The massive elephant's hoof slammed onto the platform with a dull thud. Lin Jing felt the entire lecture hall tremble, the air thick with an almost tangible spiritual pressure. Students in the front rows gasped in surprise; some even fell from their seats. Lin Jing gripped the armrests tightly, her nails digging into the wood.

The mammoth let out a low roar, the sound wave sweeping across the entire area as if it were a tangible force. Moments later, the giant beast's body began to shrink, and the gray robe once again covered the old professor's form. He straightened his collar, as if he had merely displayed an ordinary teaching aid.

“This is the Giant Form,” the professor said calmly. “It is a realm that many of you will never reach in your entire lives. But even if you do reach it, please remember—it is merely a means, not an end.”

When the bell rang, Lin Jing was still somewhat dazed. He followed the crowd out of the lecture hall, the early autumn sun shining on him, dispelling the last trace of chill from the spiritual pressure.

He hadn't walked far along the stone path when a commotion suddenly broke out ahead.

Several young nobles dressed in magnificent robes stood by the roadside. Leading them was a young man in his early twenties with long, dark blue hair tied in a ponytail and a face so handsome it was almost perfect. The level of refinement he exuded was clearly the product of a high-level spiritual bloodline—high-spiritual parents are more likely to give birth to high-spiritual offspring; the influence of bloodline on talent is an undeniable fact.

A mortal servant knelt before the young man, trembling all over, his forehead pressed against the ground.

"I...I just made a mistake for a moment..."

"Negligence?" The young man looked down at the servant, his voice carrying a nonchalant indifference. "Do you know how much this bottle of spirit dew is worth?"

"Please spare my life, young master..."

The young man sighed and held up one finger.

Lin Jing saw the servant's body become ethereal in an instant, his skin turning translucent, as if all color had been drained from him. A look of terror froze on his face, his pupils losing focus. He let out a silent scream—Lin Jing could even feel the spiritual resonance of that scream.

Then he quieted down, kneeling in place in a semi-transparent state. A spirit servant. The young man had turned a living person into a spirit servant.

Several nobles around him chuckled softly, as if they had witnessed an amusing joke. The young man carefully put away the jade bottle and turned to leave.

“The city lord’s mansion will handle the aftermath,” he said to his entourage. “Record it according to the rules and pay the corresponding price.”

Lin Jing stood there, looking at the mortal kneeling in place, now in a semi-transparent form, and a chill ran through his chest. He suddenly felt that the mortal wasn't dead, but was already "gone."

This thought made him stop in his tracks. Suddenly, the professor's words from class resurfaced in his mind, about the difference between spiritual cultivators and mortals. He hadn't explicitly stated it, but Lin Jing now fully understood its meaning: spiritual cultivators, in a sense, were no longer human. Their coldness towards ordinary people wasn't deliberate cruelty, but rather stemmed from a deep, almost insurmountable alienation.

When the power gap is deep enough, even sympathy loses its meaning. The way that young man handled this "accident" was like an ordinary person stepping on an ant; perhaps he could simply pay a small road administration fine and get away with it.

Lin Jing looked at her shadow, which was stretched long by the sunlight, and did not turn around again.
