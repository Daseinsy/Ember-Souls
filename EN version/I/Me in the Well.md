# 5 Me in the Well

Lin Jing sometimes felt that the fact that her courtyard was opposite Xuejian's was like some kind of clumsy coincidence.

A shallow stream runs between them, its sound so faint it's almost inaudible, only seeping in through the window cracks late at night.

He stood by the courtyard gate, his gaze passing over the few stones blackened by moisture, and could see the light shining from the window opposite. Most of the time, that light was on, a warm yellow, almost dim in the face of the burning sun and the ever-burning torches of the academy.

He rarely went to look at it intentionally. It was just that the courtyard was too close, so close that it felt like fate was mocking him silently.

The day they met at the academy, he was squatting on the bank, watching the flowing river, completely absorbed. He heard footsteps, looked up, and saw Xuejian standing not far away, backlit, her face somewhat blurred, only a few strands of hair sticking up visible. He lowered his head, just like when he first passed through the academy's main gate and saw that breathtaking beauty, almost forgetting to breathe for a moment.

"The White Stone Avenue is as wide as the River of Oblivion, so white that stepping on it would seem to disturb her breath. Ancient trees tower beside the stone pillars on both sides, vines entwined, and a bluish light seeps from the veins of the leaves, flickering and shimmering, breathing in harmony with the misty breeze. Fountains and corridors intertwine haphazardly, water droplets rising and shattering, refracting into a faint gold in the light, like the fleeting glimmer in her eyes when she turns her head. Spiritual light floats among the tree canopies, flashing gently and then disappearing. The entire academy lies in the light, pure and flawless, breathing long and even, as if even the wind does not wish to disturb her dream."

Grand, classical, divine. These words lined up and popped into his head, yet none of them felt quite right; he felt that even language would ruin this beauty.

"Hey, Wood, haven't I seen you somewhere before?" Xuejian asked.

Lin Jing paused for a moment, then simply nodded. Xue Jian took a few steps closer and squatted down opposite him. The stream flowed between them, and dappled sunlight fell into her eyes. "Perhaps we met in a past life." She then laughed, a soft laugh like the wind rustling through bamboo leaves.

Lin Jing didn't know what to say. Sometimes he felt ashamed of his awkward silences in social situations, and sometimes he felt that it was his unique nature.

Xuejian didn't mind. She extended her hand and said, "Let's exchange contact information." Lin Jing paused for a moment, then gave her psionic code. A gentle touch in her spiritual sense, like a fingertip touching the surface of water, caused ripples to spread out very faintly, and her node lit up.

Xuejian said, "Alright, from now on we're friends."

Lin Jing nodded. Friend. This word settled in his heart like a very light leaf, one he dared not press down on.

He said goodbye and turned to leave. Like a wounded little animal, he built walls around himself with coldness and isolation to avoid being hurt again. But that code always shone in a corner of his consciousness, faint yet clear, like a light glimpsed in the distance during a night voyage.

Lin Jing's own yard was not big. When he went back and opened the door, there was a half-dead tree in the corner of the yard.

Some students from the upper city were accompanied by well-dressed spirit servants who took care of their daily needs, attended classes on their behalf, and delivered tokens of faith.

Lin Jing didn't. The standard-issue spirit puppet provided by the academy stood in the corner, its joints gleaming with a cold silver light. He only gave it commands when necessary.

Gamma occasionally perched on his shoulder. It was the first spirit beast Lin Jing had summoned, tilting its head and looking at him with bewildered eyes. This yellow, puppy-like creature flew somewhat erratically, now stumbling and learning about this unfamiliar world.

"It is said that spiritual cultivators who can summon spirit beasts are all promising," he said self-deprecatingly, as he began to make comparisons again.

The days passed by in his spiritual practice. Apart from necessary matters, he practically kept himself glued to the courtyard.

That day, he was cultivating in the courtyard and forgot the time. He sat cross-legged, exhaling and guiding his spiritual energy along his spiritual meridians again and again. The runes on the paper were slightly warm, and the ink seemed to come alive as it slowly spread.

Every minute, every second, Lin Jing could hear his own heartbeat. The gradual increase in his spiritual energy gave him a sense of pleasure. He craved this growth, like a drowning person desperately trying to grasp something definite.

When he looked up again, it was already dark. His stomach felt empty and weak, and his fingers trembled as he turned the pages. He closed the book, pressed the rune against the corner of the desk, and got up to leave.

Before leaving, I glanced across the street. Xuejian's light was still on, a warm yellow hue reflected on the stream, stretching into a gently swaying beam.

Stepping out of the courtyard gate, the night wind rushed towards her. The embers of the fire seeped from the ground, the pillars, and even the air, illuminating the entire academy as if it were daytime, leaving no place for shadows to hide. Lin Jing knew this was to suppress the invasion of evil spirits.

He walked along the stone path. In the distance, special forces soldiers, clad in black armor, marched in perfect unison, like a silent tide gliding past the courtyard gate. Above, the Giant of Light's Heavenly Eye remained ever-open. Lin Jing never looked up, but he could feel that gaze omnipresent, silently watching him, and watching all things unsettled.

He quickened his pace, turning through several corridors, until the outline of the spirit beast stables emerged in the night. The caretaker remained seated in the old wicker chair, glancing up at him: "So late again."

Lin Jing responded.

"You new students, you forget about time when you start cultivating." The administrator tossed the fodder in his hand into the trough. "Alright, go on."

Lin Jing mounted the eagle-like mythical beast, which flapped its wings and soared into the sky, causing the ground to suddenly sink. The night wind rushed into her sleeves, and a chill crept up her spine.

He looked down and saw the lights on the ground transform into a flowing golden river, meandering slowly between the buildings. Just how vast was this academy? Perhaps no one had ever truly explored it all. He thought, adjusting his collar.

The spirit beast slowly descended. They had arrived at the dining hall. It was a single-story building, extremely large, with a porch wide enough for dozens of spirit beasts to ride side-by-side. The first time Lin Jing came, she looked up at the dome until her neck ached; later she learned it was designed for giants to eat.

As he walked in, he still felt like a little sparrow who had mistakenly wandered into a temple. The food was served by standardized spirit dolls, all silver-white, their movements mechanical and precise. Lin Jing handed him the menu.

"Do you want it spicy?"

"Less."

The puppet's spoon paused in mid-air, scooping out deep red sauce from another bucket and drizzling it over the food. Behind a silver-gray partition was the VIP area, where real people served the food, the lights shining brighter than outside, like another world.

Lin Jing had never been inside before. He carried his plate to a small round table in the single-person area and sat down. The food was mushy and didn't have much flavor; he was just trying to fill his stomach.

"one person?"

Lin Jing looked up and saw a boy who called himself Yu Ze sitting opposite her. He was tall and thin, with slightly chubby cheeks and dimples when he smiled, giving off a very friendly vibe.

"You're from the lower city area too, right?"

Lin Jing paused.

“Your demeanor, and this unworthy feeling,” Yuze said, “is very kind.”

Yuze smiled and continued, saying that some of the top elites from the upper city could directly enter the teaching building. A few days ago, he saw a parent walk into a hidden door and pass through without any obstacles, with the spirit puppet leading the way. As for those from the lower city, they were stopped outside the school gate and questioned again and again. In the end, they were only allowed to leave a message, and the spirit puppet had to deliver the items.

When it comes to class assignments, those in the Upper District have the spiritual artifacts at home to cushion their fate, ensuring they get into better classes and have better mentors; those in the Lower District start from the very bottom. The academy claims to treat everyone equally, but in reality, it clearly distinguishes between classes in every aspect.

How did you pay for your tuition?

Lin Jing was silent for a moment: "It was raised by relatives."

“It’s the same in my family. My aunt took out all her dowry. She said that once she gets into this academy, she’ll be a superior person.” Yuze finished speaking and leaned back in his chair. “That’s how it is in the lower district. Families put all their savings into their talented children, hoping that they can climb up and then help the whole family out.”

Lin Jing nodded. Yu Ze talked about other things, like interesting anecdotes, and he responded half-heartedly.

Suddenly, Lin Jing's gaze wavered, as if pulled away by something unseen. The viscous, watery sound deep within his consciousness resurfaced, gently surging against his eardrums. He snapped back to reality, lowered his head, and stirred the plate.

Yuze stared at him: "What's wrong with you?"

"It's nothing," Lin Jing said with a forced smile, "I'm just tired from cultivation."

The other person, unsuspecting, reached out and patted his shoulder, grinning mischievously, "Even cultivation requires restraint!"

But only Lin Jing herself knew that she had almost been pulled into the well again.

He never told anyone that he could see the well. It was an extremely deep, dark well, hidden at the bottom of his soul. He never dared to look down, but he could always vaguely hear the sound of viscous water from the deepest part of the ground.

He once knew a man named Ah Chen. Ah Chen could also see wells. One day, Ah Chen revealed it, but was later taken away by a man in black. After returning, he never spoke of it again.

But Lin Jing read the answer in his eyes: the Kingdom of God fears people like this, because those who can see the well can see the ugliness in people's hearts, including the unspeakable existence.

He lowered his eyes, suppressed those thoughts, and tried to get his breathing back to a normal rhythm.

Just then, Yuze lowered his voice and leaned closer: "Look at those spiritual cultivators, aren't they supposed to be able to abstain from grains? Why are they here to eat?"

Lin Jing followed his gaze and saw several spiritual cultivators sitting in the distance, their postures upright, with food laid out in front of them.

"I have a sweet tooth."

"What if you eat it?"

"I circulate my spiritual energy to burn it," Lin Jing said in a low voice, not daring to look directly at him. "It disappears as soon as it gets slightly hot."

Yuze raised an eyebrow: "That's quite something." He clicked his tongue twice, "Satisfying his cravings without being tainted by worldly concerns."

Lin Jing didn't reply. He once saw someone fall from a high ladder in the library. The person was caught in mid-air by a spirit rune, but was unconscious and pale. The academy later rescued the person. But the behavior was obviously very different.

He didn't understand at the time, but he gradually came to understand later that the gods of the Kingdom of God practiced equal exchange; if you wanted to take something back, you had to give something in return.

We can save lives, but it won't be for nothing. That's fair, isn't it?

But suddenly he felt no appetite and pushed his plate aside. His gaze swept over the sparsely populated people in the cafeteria—spiritual cultivators, freshmen eating in silence, and the VIP area where laughter occasionally broke out. Everyone was living their lives to the fullest under this seemingly free dome.

The college has no timetable, and everyone is free at all times, but there are standards for each stage. Those who do not meet the standards will be expelled, quietly, like a drop of water evaporating from the fire.

Everyone struggles in freedom, which is like a bottomless pool of water; you have to swim in it yourself, or you'll sink.

Lin Jing stood up and said goodbye to Yu Ze. Yu Ze waved his hand without looking up.

It was late at night. Lin Jing returned to the courtyard and covered the lights in the house with a special gauze, plunging the room into complete darkness. The only sound was the soft, continuous sound of the stream seeping in through the cracks in the window.

He couldn't sleep. He could sense the many people around him, their loneliness, pride, excitement, and bewilderment at their impending fall, surging like an undercurrent in the brightly lit night, flowing past him. He himself was just a stone among them, washed over by the water, yet untouched by any single drop.

He turned over, his gaze passing over the windowsill. The outline of the opposite courtyard was hidden in the night, and the windows were pitch black. Xuejian's light had been turned off sometime ago. He stared for a while, then pulled the blanket up higher. Fine, I'll sleep.

He closed his eyes. Darkness pressed in from all sides, and his consciousness plummeted into that familiar deep well.
