#  Evolution

Evolution, in essence, is the process by which an individual improves themselves and enters a theoretically higher form. For example, the cultivation of spiritual cultivators is a form of evolution. Spiritual beasts also evolve, but evolution represents destruction and rebirth. It is not painless, and it is quite lengthy. Most spiritual cultivators are like fruits that are ripened too quickly; although they have the form of a higher-level life form, their inner selves are weak and superficial.

The Great Moroccan sword of morality and ethics hangs over humanity. If your parents and your wife and children all fell into the water at the same time, and you could only save one, who would you save first? If a train was about to cross two tracks, one with a person and the other with a group of people, which way would you steer the train? If sacrificing yourself could preserve the flame of humanity, what would you choose?

This is an unsolvable paradox because no matter what choice you make, it won't be perfect. No matter what choice you make, you can find a plethora of reasons and refutations, and from different perspectives, each one seems reasonable. The solution is actually quite simple: since every choice is wrong, why not choose at all? Why burden yourself with the fate of the species and the planet? Shouldn't this be a shared responsibility? This inaction isn't about being aloof or indifferent; it's about doing what you can within your capabilities, following your conscience. I'm not trying to choose one path, because I can't choose, but I can do my best to slow down the event. If your parents, wife, and children fall into the water at the same time, you can call professionals, distribute ropes, and go to the closest one first. Since the train is bound to move, first consider whether there's a way to stop it; if not, let it continue on its course. If sacrificing myself can save all of humanity, then the first thing to question is what would destroy all of humanity, not the issue of sacrifice itself.

Some say that those who maintained dignity and principles during evolution, unwilling to abandon their individuality, ultimately failed to pass on their genes, becoming losers. However, evolution is an objective process. Why should we define the continuation of genes as success? Genes give birth to independent individuals, new life, not the continuation of anyone else's life. Does nature consider leaving offspring a success and not having offspring a failure? If people believe that leaving offspring is success, what reward does nature give to those who do? Is the reward that offspring are the continuation of one's spirit, that offspring can inherit one's family business, use one's surname, and offer incense? But isn't all of this just self-indulgent fantasy in people's minds?
Furthermore, biological evolution follows the principle of survival of the fittest, not survival of the strongest. This means that genes that survive the evolutionary process are either better adapted to the environment or simply survived a major catastrophe by sheer luck. It doesn't mean that the most advanced, fastest, or strongest organisms will prevail in evolution. In other words, nature doesn't care how strong you are; nature doesn't even have the concept of strength or weakness. It simply follows a set of cold, mechanical laws: you survive if you can survive, and you don't survive if you can't.
