# The Road of Darkness

For the Kingdom of God to move forward, it must continuously draw wealth from the wilderness and the outside world. Lin Jing had read about this in his textbook during his first year of school. But the textbook didn't tell him where that wealth actually came from.

Until he first stood on the top floor of the college tower, looking down beyond the walls.

There was no sun in the wilderness. It was an eternal night, like an inverted pot covering the entire earth. In the far distance, there were faint points of light, but those lights were not warm; they even carried a seductive chill. He later learned that those lights were mostly not a good omen. Evil spirits, even demons, lingered there.

But in that boundless darkness, there were several paths.

The Holy Spirits opened paths for all living beings with their lives. These paths, like ribbons of light, stretched into the depths of darkness, connecting city-state after city. As long as one walked along these paths, the light dispelled the darkness, and monsters would not approach. Thus, people from different kingdoms and different cities were finally able to cross the wilderness and communicate with one another. Caravans, envoys, and students traveled along these paths of light. The economic lifeline of the entire kingdom rested on these thoroughfares paved by the command of the Holy Spirit.

Standing atop the tower, Lin Jing felt the sound of the well suddenly seem more distant. Gazing at the roads, he recalled someone saying: "The way is the remnant of the Holy Spirit, and the light is the indestructible will."

He remembered those words for a long time.

Xuejian never asked about his past. Lin Jing found this strange, because most people would be curious about someone whose depths are hard to fathom. But Xuejian wasn't. She would simply sit down beside him, occasionally handing him a cup of hot tea, and sometimes saying nothing.

Lin Jing knew that Xuejian was a geisha. News travels fast in the academy, and people with such backgrounds are bound to be talked about to some extent. But Xuejian seemed used to it. She rarely took the initiative to approach others, always standing on the edge of the crowd. But her sense of smell was very sharp, not for scents, but for emotions. She said she could sense a hidden sadness in Lin Jing, like a piece of paper folded many times, the surface smoothed out, but the creases still there.

Lin Jing neither admitted nor denied it.

He treated Xuejian the same as always, without showing her any special consideration because of her background. Xuejian later said that just because of this, she felt that Lin Jing was different from others.

That day, Lin Jing was returning to the courtyard from the lecture hall when she saw Xue Jian being stopped on the road by someone.

He was a young nobleman, tall and slender, wearing the academy's uniform, with silver threads embellishing the cuffs and collar. His hair was light blonde, like a lamp in the evening light. His face was handsome, especially his eyes, an excessively blue, like meticulously polished gems. Lin Jing recognized him; Xavier, from a renowned spiritual family that owned vast fiefdoms in the central part of the divine kingdom.

“Miss Xuejian, my proposal still stands.” Xavier’s voice was polite, even gentle. “As my maid, you will no longer have to do those chores. You will receive a fixed monthly salary, and I will provide the resources you need for spiritual practice. I can even try to get you the opportunity to sit in on my sessions. Please consider it.”

Xuejian shook her head.

“Follow me, and you’ll have somewhere to go after you leave the academy,” Xavier said unhurriedly. “Otherwise, once you leave the academy, it won’t be easy for someone like you, a geisha, to make a living with just that little bit of talent.”

This statement didn't sound like a threat; it sounded like a cold, hard fact.

Xuejian simply raised her head, looked at him, and said calmly, "Young Master Xavier, your offer is very good, but I am not willing."

“If you don’t want to be a maid, you can have another title.” Xavier still smiled, her blue eyes deepening. “I don’t like to force people, especially not someone like you.”

He paused, then added, "You know, many people don't have that opportunity after leaving the academy."

Lin Jing understood the implication in those words. Inside the academy walls was safe, but outside, away from the protection of teachers and rules, no one could guarantee what would happen to someone without a foundation. Xavier was reminding her, or rather, using "good intentions" to remind her.

Xuejian remained unmoved.

“I know,” she said, “but I still don’t want to.”

Xavier stared at her for a long time. Lin Jing even thought he might do something, but in the end, the blue-eyed nobleman just smiled and took a step back.

"Alright. But I welcome you to change your mind anytime."

He turned and left, his silver-threaded sleeves swaying gently in the wind.

Lin Jing stood still until Xavier was far away before slowly walking to Xuejian's side. Xuejian was looking down, her fingers unconsciously twisting the hem of her clothes. She noticed him coming, looked up, and smiled slightly.

"Did you hear that?"

"I heard you," Lin Jing said.

“Don’t worry,” Xuejian said. “He won’t do anything openly. The academy’s rules are there, and every student has a status. The gods follow the principle of equivalent exchange. If he were to forcibly harm me, the price he would have to pay would be something even a nobleman couldn’t afford.”

She paused, then added, "Or even if he can afford it, his parents will still go bankrupt and beat him half to death."

Lin Jing smiled slightly, amused by her remark. Xue Jian's eyes brightened a little when she saw him smile.

"How did you end up here?" she asked.

"I just happened to be passing by," Lin Jing said.

He didn't say that he was annoyed by the sound of the well and wanted to go for a walk. Nor did he mention that her composed demeanor when she stopped Xavier reminded him of the paths he had seen from the tower. Surrounded by darkness, yet always illuminated by light.

The two walked back side by side. The wind blew from outside the academy, carrying the indescribable chill of the wasteland. On the distant high walls, the patrolling spirit lanterns flickered, making the roads paved with lives look even more like silent rivers.

Xuejian suddenly said, "Lin Jing, have you heard the sounds of those roads?"

"No," Lin Jing said. "Do they make sounds?"

“Yes. It’s very soft, like many people singing.” Xuejian turned her head to look at him, her eyelashes like two thin lines in the twilight. “When I was little, I was found at the gate of the orphanage. The teacher said that someone put me on the ground, and I was only wrapped in a piece of cloth. I don’t remember what my parents look like, but there is always a gentle voice in my dreams, like singing, or like whispering. Sometimes I wonder if that voice is my parents.”

She smiled, a hint of self-deprecation in her voice: "It's a very clichéd script of destiny, isn't it? Maybe my parents are some holy spirit, or even a god."

Lin Jing didn't laugh. He suddenly felt that Xuejian's dream and his own had a similar shape in some unseen place.

"Maybe," Lin Jing said.

Xuejian glanced at him with some surprise.

Don't you think I'm arrogant?

“Everyone should have a moment of self-confidence,” Lin Jing said. “It’s better than giving up too early.”

Xuejian didn't reply, but simply exhaled softly, as if she were expelling something very heavy.

The two reached a fork in the road. Xuejian went left, and Lin Jing went right.

"See you tomorrow," Xuejian said.

"See you tomorrow," Lin Jing said.

He walked a short distance and turned back to see Xuejian's figure standing in the twilight, slender and thin, yet standing very straight. The wind blew her clothes, like a path of light in the dark night, quiet yet carrying a light that refused to be extinguished.
