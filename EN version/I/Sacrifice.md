#  Sacrifice

In those ancient city-states, people held an annual "silent memorial"—no light, no music, no offerings, only the entire town gathering before dawn, standing silently in the square, facing east. They didn't know the names of the fallen heroes, nor to whom those souls belonged, but every living life owed something to those who were no longer there. Some were forgotten in life, but after death, they upheld this land.

Those who carry firewood for the masses should not be allowed to freeze to death in the snow. This saying is inscribed on the gates of many cities. Ironically, however, the very people who inscribe this saying are often the ones who push those carrying firewood into the snow. History always cycles in this way, repeatedly presenting another choice, but this does not mean that the other choice will be brighter.
