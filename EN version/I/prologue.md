#  prologue

The sun went out.

Everything ended with Ragnarok, when the gods, in their quest to become the sole supreme ruler, led all living beings in an unprecedented war.

But this war of the gods ultimately had no winners. The continent was dismembered, leaving deep, bone-revealing wounds; the heavens wept, pouring countless seas down from the sky; and countless lives perished tragically in the tragedy.

The once invincible gods also lost their original power due to the depletion of the faith of the masses.

Demons crawled out of the abyss wounds in an endless stream, evil spirits twisted in agony, and sighed with satisfaction as they slaughtered.

The monsters first targeted the weakened gods, who vanished one by one under their siege.

The sun thus fell in that terrible twilight, and the world was plunged into eternal darkness ever since.

Their former homes are now ruins, and the remaining humans place their hopes on the rising shelters, now filling the gaps in their defenses with their own flesh and blood.

The monster's scythe mercilessly tore through the air, and powerful evil spirits rode skeletal warhorses, trampling over severed limbs. Young knights and boy soldiers who had just learned to wield guns fell helplessly like weeds.

They fell down, slowly yet quickly.

Screams echoed across the land, like the struggles of livestock being butchered, only to be shredded in the end. The sounds of tearing, screams, and the cracking of bones breaking mingled together, as if the land itself was weeping, screaming, and silently swallowing them up.

"It hurts so much... hurry..." Some soldiers struggled on their severed limbs, but their tenacious will to live did not allow them to die immediately. They could only groan in pain and reach out to their comrades with trembling hands.

He wasn't the first to utter those words, nor would he be the last; the soldier beside him, tears welling in his eyes, swiftly severed his head with a swift stroke. Blood gushed from the severed neck like flames, soaking the earth and staining the sky crimson.

"General, are we really going to die here?" A hoarse voice rang out. It was Kyle, a soldier who was only nineteen years old. His hands were trembling, and his eyes were filled with resentment and fear. His sword had broken at some point, leaving only a bloodstained broken blade.

Allen slammed the magic-blocking golden shield, which already had a crack split in it, into the mud. The edge of the shield stuck into the soil and emitted a final barrier of spiritual light, blocking the attacks that flew from the demons.

He took a half step back, the tip of his sword against the edge of his shield. His eyes, their light already dimmed, slid over the fallen comrades. His voice was hoarse and calm, yet carried a dying tenderness: "Behind us are our families. We cannot fall. For our fallen brothers... for our elders and fellow countrymen behind us... even if we can only hold this line of defense for a second longer... we cannot retreat."

He abruptly changed his tone, his voice hardening: "Those who retreat without permission will be executed."

The soldiers, barely holding on, knelt in the mud and blood, as if facing an altar of gods. The wind howled in their ears, whipping up clouds of dust that blotted out the sky, the dark clouds hanging so low they seemed to press into their chests. Their voices, torn apart by the wind, echoed again and again:

"God...look here...please bring miracles upon this land of blood and fire..."

They were trying to grasp at their last hope.

But no god descended from the sky.

There was no miracle cast by golden holy light.

At that moment, a dense sound of footsteps came from behind, gradually approaching like a tide.

The soldiers instinctively turned to look, their eyes bloodshot, a mixture of hope and despair in their gazes—

"Is it reinforcements?... Is it the knights sent by the gods to support us?... Has a miracle finally arrived...?"

After the sound of footsteps cleaved through the darkness, they saw only a group of figures wearing cloaks and walking unsteadily.

It was the Earl, dressed in an old military uniform, his medals long since rusted; it was Grandpa Robinson, his hunched back carrying a greatsword; it was Mrs. Morren, holding in her arms the rag doll she had made for casting spells.

Behind them was a group of elderly and frail people, yet as steadfast as rocks. Some of them didn't even have sharp weapons in their hands, only hoes, iron pots, and broken wooden sticks.

"What are you doing here? You'll die!" Allen couldn't help but yell.

The count smiled at him, his wrinkles revealing tenderness: "It's alright, we've already sent the children away. Let us stay with you and hold them back a little longer... even if it's just for a little while."

The soldiers could no longer speak; their throats felt like they were filled with lead. They knew they were doomed to die, but at that moment, the embers of their long-extinguished faith were rekindled in the wind.

The wind grew stronger, swirling with blood, ashes, and memories. At that moment, time seemed to stand still.

.....................

Far behind the soldiers, on the hazy horizon, one could vaguely see women carrying children, some flying at great speed through the air, others riding on various mythical beasts, desperately rushing towards the enormous barrier of light that was gradually enlarging in the darkness.

This was originally a huge group that migrated to the shelter in the dead of night, but more and more people turned back and never returned.

The scattered evil spirits around them, like venomous snakes that have smelled blood, struggled to crawl out of the void and twisted as they rushed toward the crowd.

A young scholar wanted to stay and resist, but he was stopped and told to take his books and knowledge with him to preserve the last spark of civilization.

The few remaining men, seeing the ever-encroaching darkness, gritted their teeth, glanced at the infant child in swaddling clothes, and turned back to face death without hesitation.

All that remained were cries to his tearful relatives: "Go! Go!"

Some mothers, exhausted from running, collapsed to the ground like kites with broken strings. They simply raised their hands and murmured as they handed their children to those who still had the strength in their hands.

"My darling, I feel like I can't go on with you anymore. Please take good care of our child."

The first survivors finally crossed the edge of the shelter. Every second that passed meant a life was torn apart; that was the eternal boundary between life and death.

The dew of the night still clung to their bodies, their shoes were covered in mud, and their faces were filled with blankness and silence.

They survived, but the sweetness of their lives had been ruthlessly torn apart by the claws of fate, like a dream.

A broken wooden bird fell to the ground and was picked up by a child who was crying and hugging it tightly. It had been a talisman his mother had given him.

If viewed from a distant high altitude, such stories are unfolding in every corner of the world, and faint lights gradually appear on the dark continent, separated from each other like isolated islands, providing the last remaining light for a world on its way to its end.

Above one of the shelters, a dark golden flame burned. In a slightly dark corner, an elderly scholar with white hair stood silently for a long time, his hand trembling as he raised the carving knife, its blade leaving bloody marks, and inscribed lines of text on the nameless boulder:

"That wasn't a miracle from God."

"That was a flame that people themselves ignited, bit by bit, with their blood and lives."

"Those who fell did not receive the blessing of God."

"They themselves have become gods to others."

He paused, then engraved a line of smaller characters at the end:

"I once hoped that miracles would descend upon every corner of weeping, but later I realized that it is not that there are no miracles in the world, but that most miracles are drowned in the silence of the masses."

Far away from him, a being whose face was obscured was shrouded in the light of dusk, sending a soul-stirring echo through the crowd with its spiritual power:

“Welcome to the new world,” He said.
