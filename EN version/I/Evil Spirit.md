#  Evil Spirit

Does becoming an evil spirit mean eternal damnation? Not necessarily. A very small number of evil spirits are able to break free from their bonds and regain their intelligence and freedom; the "Order of Evil Spirits" is one such example. Legend has it that the Order of Evil Spirits cooperates very closely with the Faceless Men, perhaps out of a shared sense of suffering.

Evil spirits are not external enemies, but rather the karmic fire within humanity itself.

Lin Jing had personally witnessed the tragic state of people possessed by evil spirits. First, they would behave abnormally and fear the light, then they would become mentally deranged and even launch destructive attacks, and finally, they would exhaust their energy and become emaciated.

Therefore, what is compassion? If there are not enough means to deal with the infection, ending it early is the greatest compassion.

Legend has it that the souls of those who die of old age ascend to the heavens, becoming part of the shining stars. However, red stars are seen as an ominous sign. This is the last surviving continent after the destruction of the universe. Different continents, each a remnant of a dying civilization; this is the burial ground of countless civilizations. The stars are not in the sky, for there is nothing there. Even if you fly to the ends of the earth, you will find yourself back where you started. Stars have fallen to the continents, sunk into the deep sea; the scattered campfires and distant flames are proof of the shattered.

Life is a chance miracle, yet incredibly fragile. Here, time has no meaning, for the flow of time varies from place to place. But regardless of the flow of time, the total time experienced by everyone is the same. Countless civilizations have shattered, leaving only tattered remnants. For even the God of nature has died in the twilight of the gods. The world no longer has a sun; man-made fire is the only sun.

Some places still resemble paradise, while just beyond the not-too-distant border lies a desolate wasteland. Yet, civilization is unwilling to simply die—if it can live well, no life would want to die.

The town, the poor. They stare wide-eyed, frantically dodging oncoming trucks; or they toil numbly in the mines, working day and night. He lost his mother at a young age—she went insane, becoming a demon. His father, falsely accused and imprisoned years earlier, was forced to work in the mines, sending only meager savings each month. His grandparents, unable to bear seeing him suffer, took him from the slums and raised him.

By the way, this is a working-class neighborhood, not a slum, but it's not wealthy either. That's why he can still go to school. Even with nothing, people still fight tooth and nail to attend school, hoping to learn enough skills to manipulate spiritual energy, cultivate successfully, and eventually become a great figure. But becoming a great figure is never that simple.

Those who achieve mastery in cultivation can become lords, wielding immense power, capable of fighting alone or manipulating spiritual relics to combat evil spirits. In the center of the great city, a pile of spiritual relics is displayed, hoping someone can resonate with them . Those lucky enough to resonate are all recruited by the various powerful families. Every child, upon reaching adulthood, yearns to be that chosen one. But he, in the end, was not so fortunate.

This novel is almost entirely devoid of trivial, tedious, or padded details; it's packed with substantial content, a true breath of fresh air in the literary world. Despite its abundance of substance, the pacing is unhurried, allowing you to continue reading from any page. Every sentence is imbued with emotion, igniting the reader's passion. It almost completely eliminates all the cumbersome rhetoric, ornamentation, and superficiality found in literature, achieving a level of purity that is unparalleled. Even when devoting considerable space to describing plot, it focuses solely on depicting beautiful scenery and state of mind, making for a remarkably comfortable reading experience. This has truly pioneered a new genre in literature, transforming reading into a pure enjoyment, devoid of the tedious descriptions and clichés commonly found in literature.
