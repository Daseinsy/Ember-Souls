#  Totem

After their initial awakening, each person can visualize their own totem in their mind. Totems are divided into five levels: mythological totem, legendary totem, holy spirit totem, immortal totem, and celestial totem. The level of a totem is not fixed; as the practitioner grows and encounters different experiences, the totem itself may undergo qualitative changes. When activating the power of a totem, a corresponding totem mark will form on the practitioner's forehead. This is a sign of manifested power and proof of identity.

Each person, based on their own experiences and spiritual qualities, can develop a unique "title." Only those who have reached a certain level of cultivation can possess a title; such cultivators are called titled cultivators. Titles can be concealed at will, but certain specific spiritual totems can penetrate the barriers of cultivation levels and directly reveal the hidden title—this is the preciousness of spiritual totems and the reason why major powers vie to recruit such talents.

Besides personal totems, nations also possess national totems. Several major federations each possess their own totem: Azure Dragon, Fierce Tiger, Flaming Phoenix, and Breathing Tortoise. A national totem is an abstract entity, existing independently of individual will, but rather embodying the collective will of millions of people. When evil spirits or demonic beasts launch a massive invasion, threatening the survival of civilization, the national totem manifests in the world according to the people's will. At that time, the totems of all cultivators within the nation will leave imprints on their bodies; the number and rank of these imprints directly affect the strength of the national totem. Therefore, protecting endangered totems—especially ancient totems that were once glorious but now have no successors—has become an important public service. The Totem Protection Association (referred to as the Protection Association) specializes in this work because totems must have a corresponding existence in the Mirror Realm; they cannot arise from nothing.

Another everyday application of totems is transportation. Huge animal-shaped stone sculptures stand in city squares; travelers place their palms on their bases, silently recite their destination, and the totems awaken. Some transform into wind birds soaring through the sky, others into earth dragons burrowing into the soil, and still others unfold like floating jellyfish, gently swaying their tentacles in the rivers. The more mana injected, the more refined the totem's transformation—a nobleman's totem can become a six-winged golden roc, while a totem in the slums can only barely transform into a three-legged beast, needing to rest after every three steps. No one can explain why totems respond to human will. Craftsmen say the patterns contain ancient runes, priests say the stone comes from bones from before the world's creation, while skeptics privately discuss: perhaps the totems never moved at all, but only our perception of space shifted.
