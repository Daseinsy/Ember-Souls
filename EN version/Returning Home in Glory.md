#  Returning Home in Glory

There was a smell of urine in the alley, mixed with coal ash and overnight rainwater, sticky and sticky on the bluestone slabs.

Lin Jing didn't go in. She stood at the alley entrance, her spiritual senses like a thin layer of mist, slowly seeping into the narrow alley. An old man was dozing against the wall, a bundle of cardboard he had collected leaning against his side. Further in, the wooden sign for the relief station leaned against the third door, the lettering faded and white from the rain.

Ah Chen stood on the threshold, holding a rough pottery bowl.

"That's all?" he said.

The butler leaned against the doorframe, head down clipping his nails, not looking at him. "If you think it's too little, don't come."

Ah Chen didn't speak. The thin porridge in the bowl was so clear that his own face was reflected in it; he was so thin that his cheekbones protruded. He pursed his lips and took a step back from the threshold.

The deacon looked up and chuckled, "I think you should try the mines in the west of the city. Maybe you can find something to eat there. I just don't know if your body can handle it."

Ah Chen's hand tightened slightly; the rim of the bowl was hot. He said nothing, but slowly bent down, intending to place the bowl on the stone steps.

Lin Jing finished reading it.

She raised her hand and whistled. The sound was not loud, but clear, like a thin wire cutting through the alley.

A large, silver-gray wolf emerged from behind her and slowly entered the alley. Its four paws pattered softly on the flagstones. The alley was narrow, and the wolf's shoulders and back almost brushed against the walls. It didn't howl or bare its teeth; it simply walked slowly, carrying with it the dry, distinctive scent of a wild animal.

Upon hearing the sound, the deacon paused for a moment, then sprang up from the door frame.

"Oh, Miss Lin Jing." He quickly put on a smile, his voice softened, and he bent down lower than the threshold. "What brings you here? This alley is filthy, look at this..."

Lin Jing didn't respond to him, but rode the wolf past at a moderate pace. As the wolf passed the entrance of the relief station, she turned her head and her gaze fell on A-Chen.

"Achen," she called out.

Ah Chen looked up, still holding the bowl in his hand. He looked at her, his gaze slightly evasive, but he didn't look away.

"Grandma made soup today," Lin Jing said. "Let's go."

Her tone was flat, as gray and dull as the alley, revealing no emotion. But Ah Chen understood. His Adam's apple bobbed, but he didn't speak. He looked down at the bowl in his hand, then glanced at the steward.

The butler immediately smiled and waved his hand, "Put the bowl down, put it down. I'll have someone clean it up later." He then turned to Lin Jing, "Miss Lin Jing, take care, I'll see you another day..."

Before he could finish speaking, Lin Jing gently squeezed the wolf's belly. The giant wolf took a few steps, like a quiet big dog, and slowly walked towards the other end of the alley. Achen put down his bowl and followed. He walked a little quickly, his shoes making a soft scraping sound on the ground, as if afraid of being left behind in this alley.

The alleyway fell silent again.

The deacon stood at the door, a smile still lingering on his face, before slowly fading as he wiped his face with his sleeve. He glanced at Ah Chen's retreating figure, as if looking at someone who had suddenly become troublesome.

"That's really..." he muttered under his breath, not finishing his sentence.

From then on, Ah Chen's begging bowl would always have a few extra pieces of meat.
