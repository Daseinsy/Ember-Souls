# 1 Dusk Train

The station was bustling with people, most of them standing like servants, waiting for the train to arrive.

A train appeared on the distant horizon, its entire body a glassy color. In the glow of the setting sun, it seemed to melt into golden wings, slowly flying down from the sky.

A cold, synthesized voice broadcasted: "Heavenly Train 2 is about to arrive at the station. Passengers, please be aware of safety."

The psychic staff, dressed in black and white uniforms, directed the psychic puppets to drive away those still lingering near the platform.

Lin Jing stood in the crowd, his short black hair flowing, receiving a homemade jianbing (Chinese crepe) from his grandmother. Lin Jing wasn't actually hungry, but he still took a bite. The familiar flavor, with its scallion aroma and smoky notes, burst in his mouth, making his eyes slightly moist.

Grandma carried the old, patched red velvet bag, listing out his daily necessities as she nagged: "You must take care of yourself and drink more water."

"Xiao Lin is a very gifted child," the relatives around him always said, their eager eyes fixed on him. "He will definitely become a powerful spiritual cultivator."

“There are really not many children who can go to college,” an auntie chattered. “We will do everything in our power to support his schooling, even if it means pooling the resources of our entire clan.”

"If Xiaolin becomes a powerful spiritual cultivator in the future, never forget your third uncle."

He listened, nodding, pretending to be seriously packing his luggage. He had an ability to see through most pretense and masks, and the world was always so noisy that he only felt the noise.

Just as the train was about to land, a ragged, disheveled figure darted out from the crowd, stumbling and falling towards the tracks like a bird with broken wings. Those around gasped and quickly retreated to make way.

The train relentlessly ran over it, the powerful shockwave exploding into a cloud of blood mist—yet the sturdy glass body remained unmarked, the train didn't even sway, and came to a steady stop on the platform.

A human spiritualist administrator arrived at the scene, seemingly muttering something under his breath. With a wave of his hand, a bag of spiritual objects flew out. A precise and irresistible suction force emanated from the bag, drawing all the flesh and carcasses on the tracks into it.

The people on the platform watched all of this with indifference, with only a few showing expressions of pity and pain.

The train attendants stood in each carriage, ready to verify identities.

Lin Jing was greeted by a psionic doll. It glanced at his uniform issued by the academy, which bore his enrollment number, and then its expressionless face revealed a human-like respect.

This is a world where spiritual energy is the foundation of cultivation, and powerful spiritual cultivators even possess the ability to move mountains and seas. Before, when he walked the streets in coarse linen clothes, he was met with indifference and even contempt. Now, wearing this uniform, everything has changed.

Lin Jing turned around one last time and waved goodbye to her family.

He only saw his grandmother's kind smile, her cloudy eyes filled with innocent light.

As the train started moving, Lin Jing sat by the window and looked back through the glass window—his grandmother was still holding that familiar red canvas bag, quietly standing on the platform watching him. The train gradually rose into the air, and his grandmother's figure became increasingly blurry, turning into an indistinct black dot, until finally even that dot disappeared.

His mother passed away when he was very young. Later, his father also left to work in another city, leaving behind some compensation money. Only his grandparents were left to depend on him. Later, his grandfather also passed away. He experienced death many times, and from then on, he was terrified of death itself.

"No way, could this be the last time we see each other?"

"No, we will definitely meet again."

Lin Jing murmured to herself, as if she were about to slump back in the chair, but she straightened her back.

The carriage was packed with people, most of them dressed in coarse linen clothes. Occasionally, someone would glance at him, lower their head, and reveal a complex, speechless expression, just as he had once looked at those important figures.

These gazes severed him from his past, making him feel as if he had become a different person.

But he didn't feel happy.

He recalled a sentence he had read in a book: "When poor, one should cultivate one's own virtue; when successful, one should benefit all under heaven." However, most people cannot do this because the poor are always insecure, and how can those in high positions understand the suffering of another species?

Moreover, he is neither poor nor successful. He is stuck in the middle, unable to reach either extreme.

His thoughts surged like a tide, yet he felt an emptiness within.

He didn't know who he was, where he came from, or where he was going. It was as if something was constantly pushing him in a certain direction, but he couldn't quite put his finger on what it was. He hadn't yet found the most fitting way, the sense of redemption inherent in that thing itself. Those most lovable people, those who belonged to this world, had already vanished into the trailing laser beams across the sky. What are we left with?

Lin Jing sometimes thinks about these strange questions, but he quickly suppresses them and looks up at the scenery outside the car window.

The train continued its journey at the speed of light high above the deserted sky, shrinking the landscape below smaller and smaller.

He had never seen such a magnificent sight in his life. The distant villages and trees seemed draped in a veil in the dark golden light, like an ink painting left over from an old era. Colorful flowers emitted iridescent light, falling in the wind, and the world's exquisite beauty cascaded down like a waterfall.

He fell asleep without realizing it. He was really tired. He hardly dared to sleep at home. He only dozed off for a short while before dreaming about the well again.

He seemed to be walking through an abyss, then plunging headfirst into the well, falling slowly like a feather. Legend says the underworld has countless tombstones, each inscribed with a poem, telling countless stories. No one knows where the underworld truly is; legend says it's inside a well, and everyone has a chance to dream of it. He knew it was fear, that indescribable chill creeping up from the depths of the well.

Lin Jing woke up from his dream with a shiver. Although he didn't remember what he saw in the well, he could still recall the piercing sorrow and pain.

A violent impulse rose in his chest—as if he wanted to grab something, or as if he wanted to smash something.

But he didn't tell anyone about this. Because he had seen with his own eyes how the Kingdom of God treated the children who claimed they could see wells.

Just then, he saw the girl.

She sat diagonally opposite, her face veiled, obscuring her features as if she didn't want others to recognize her. But her clear, bright eyes were captivating.

She had long, sky-red hair, a burning color, like the last glimmer of light in the twilight that refused to be extinguished.

Lin Jing's heart skipped a beat, and he smiled self-deprecatingly—he knew he was a sentimental person, and he always tended to notice beautiful things.

But this time is different.

What attracted him wasn't her indistinct face, but her unique aura. It was as if he sensed something instantly. He felt as if he had seen her somewhere before, or rather, he felt a sense of familiarity—like a kindred spirit.

His breathing became softer, and he began to relax, but at the same time, his body was like a persistent radar, frantically scanning for possible dangers around him, even if there were none.

Outside the car window, the sky was getting dark.

Beyond the barrier of the Kingdom of God lies eternal night, a desolate wasteland atop ruins; within the Kingdom of God, however, daytime is twilight—why twilight? Because the artificial sun can only display the color of twilight, or rather, to conserve spiritual energy, it is forced to emit only the minimum amount of light.

Inside the temple, after various procedures, the deacons began to gradually shut off the power supply to the artificial sun. The light in the sky grew fainter and fainter, and night watchmen, carrying lanterns, walked along the streets to remind everyone that night was approaching. Night patrols and deacons maintained order, and the children who had been playing wildly, hearing their families urging them that "it's getting dark," quickly ran back home.

This is just a city-state, but it's incredibly large, so large that it requires psionic trains for transportation, and every family has their own villa. The residential areas are vast, while the trading district is mainly located in a designated area.

The divine kingdom provides the most basic necessities of life. Even without working, everyone can survive. This is because the city-state doesn't need so many people most of the time—most work can be done by spirit puppets set up by spiritual practitioners, and many things no longer require human intervention. The only obligation of ordinary people is to pay the city-state a monthly fee of spirit stones formed from spiritual energy.

Everyone's identity is registered, and psychic communication is everywhere, like a heavenly eye covering the entire divine kingdom.

The highest profession in the divine kingdom is naturally related to the temples. Because the divine kingdom is so vast, temples are established in different regions. But the most central temple is the one at the very core—from which the artificial sun rises. The more peripheral the region, the weaker the protection, the dimmer the light, and the more dangerous it is. It is said that all collected spirit stones are transported to the temple, where they are burned to maintain the stability of the divine kingdom.

For ordinary people, sunbathing is one of the few ways to obtain psionic energy.

As the train was about to enter the Divine Realm, a psionic broadcast announced: "Passengers, please prepare for identity verification in advance."

The divine kingdom is vast, with the inner and outer regions almost like two separate worlds. Legend has it that it is a place close to the gods, so it is under extremely strict control. Even when the sun sets, a backup fire is lit to drive away the terrors in the darkness.

However, most people never have the opportunity to enter, or are only eligible to enter on occasional celebratory open days.

The temple regularly preaches, and there are large training grounds in various locations. It is said that a single large training ground can accommodate tens of thousands of people. How is order maintained? Of course, it is maintained by spiritual cultivators dispatched by the temple. Powerful spiritual cultivators can directly transform into giants of light, or have their subordinate spirit beasts or birds monitor the entire area.

A group of spiritual cultivators entered the carriage, emanating a terrifying aura—which they skillfully concealed. Behind them followed a group of psionic puppets and countless spirit beasts.

After verification, the massive city gate slowly opened, revealing the entrance to the inner city.

The train arrived at the station. The psionic broadcast echoed throughout the carriage. The girl stood up, walked through the crowd, and headed for the exit.

Lin Jing didn't follow. He simply stood up, carrying a slightly bulky backpack, and lagged far behind the crowd.

Lin Jing walked towards the exit, the night wind blowing against her face. In the distance, a giant of light stood silently. These giants of light were scattered throughout the divine kingdom, overlooking the ground like silent lighthouses.

He glanced back in the direction the girl had left.

The girl is gone, but the clear yet deep light in her eyes remains in his memory.

The backpack was a bit heavy. It contained all his luggage and the bag of cold jianbing guozi (Chinese savory crepes) that his grandmother had given him.

He took a deep breath and stepped into the darkness.

He didn't know what awaited him ahead, but he knew he had to move forward.

For that surging sea of people, and for everything that is inexplicable and inexplicable.
