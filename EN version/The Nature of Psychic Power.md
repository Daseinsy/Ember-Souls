#  The Nature of Psychic Power

Psychic energy is the primordial molecule that constitutes all matter in this world. All things in heaven and earth, from mountains and rocks to flesh and blood, from wind and thunder to thought, are based on different arrangements and condensations of psychic energy. Psychic energy is formless and intangible, yet it is omnipresent, like water seeping into sand and soil.

Thought itself is a form of power. In this world, thought cannot directly affect matter, but thought can interact directly with itself. Human intention requires some medium—the most common medium being the eyes, where the gaze reaches; the blind rely on actions, sounds, or other forms of perception to transmit their mental power. Bards transform their mental power into sound, influencing objects through rhythm. High-level practitioners can even temporarily alter the laws of physics with their mental power, but the wider and greater the impact of this alteration, the more exponentially the difficulty and energy required.

The use of mental power consumes spiritual energy, which can only be replenished through sleep. Excessive consumption leads to mental exhaustion, causing fainting in mild cases and permanent damage to the spiritual foundation in severe cases. Therefore, even the most powerful practitioners must abide by the circadian rhythm—this has nothing to do with power, but is an ironclad rule of life.

Complete chromatogram of Tian Shi Tou
The Heavenly Stones emit different colors in response to the intensity of spiritual energy, a method of detection recognized in the cultivation world, but no one knows the specific principle behind it. The complete color spectrum is divided into eight levels: white, gray, red, green, blue, purple, gold, and rainbow. These eight colors correspond to different stages from beginner to legendary, but they are not strictly linear—the same color may cover a fairly wide range, and the spiritual energy colors of some geniuses may completely transcend this classification system.
However, it must be emphasized that realms cannot be quantified or precisely defined; they can only be felt. Even if a sage is standing in front of you, you may not be able to discern just how powerful he is.

The protagonist's crimson-black aura exemplifies this. It's not any color on the color spectrum, but rather a deep crimson mixed with the pitch-black of eternal night, instantly evoking images of the abyss. This color has appeared only a handful of times in historical records, each time corresponding to a figure significant enough to alter the course of an era. When the Heavenly Stone examines the protagonist, it trembles violently, emitting not a stable aura, but a heart-pounding pulse—as if the stone itself is gripped by fear.

The unit of psychic energy is called "Mana," which is both a unit of measurement and the universal currency of this world. Each Mana coin contains a wisp of psychic energy, and the light within can be seen moving through its translucent crystal shell. Mana coins have a dual nature: they can be traded in the market for food, clothing, shelter, and transportation; they can also be directly absorbed by cultivators to refine their meridians and enhance their cultivation. The Mana coins of the poor glow as faintly as a dying ember, while those in the vaults of the wealthy are piled high with Mana, their psychic energy so concentrated that the air itself seems electrified. The strength of a spiritual cultivator largely depends on how much Mana they possess—it is both a resource for cultivation and power itself.
Psychic energy can be measured numerically, with 0 to 1000 generally considered the initial stage, and over 1 million the dominant stage. Higher values are unrecorded because no human has ever reached that level; it is considered the realm of gods. However, certain objects do exist that exhibit psychic energy values far exceeding human limits—such as 999.999999 psychic energy—these objects are usually associated with ancient deities or spiritual relics, and their very existence is a challenge to the common sense of cultivators.

Understanding the three-layer structure of ridges

A cultivator once attempted to fly into the starry sea, and his experience revealed the secret of the layered structure of the sky. The lowest layer is the "Human Layer," where spiritual energy still lingers, sustaining life, and occasionally dotted with floating islands and flying magical beasts. Above that is the "Void Layer," a deathly stillness where even the wind has ceased, and there is no trace of any life. The highest layer is the "Recognition Layer"—despite flying upwards, one finds themselves plummeting towards the ground, as if the sky were a giant mirror, and their reflection was pulling them down. The powerful cultivator eventually returned to the mountain peak from which he began, his robes fluttering, his face deathly pale, and from then on, he never spoke of that experience again. The existence of the Recognition Layer suggests that the space of this world is not flat, but rather possesses an extremely complex topological structure—what you think is exploration is actually being bounced back by the world itself.

The absolute boundary of physical laws

There are no bacteria, no viruses, and no cellular rebellion in this world. Sunlight can kiss wounded flesh directly without fear of infection, because no microscopic organisms invisible to the naked eye will multiply at the wound site. Inflammation and fever are meaningless words, existing like "vaccine" and "antibiotic" in the corners of ancient books, debated by scholars as the absurd collective hysteria of the ancients. But there is still excruciating pain when limbs are broken, and staggering when there is incoordination—the earth is not merciful because you spare it from disease; when the fangs of a predator bite into an arm, the tearing sensation is sincere and primal, without any concealment.

There was no electricity or fuel. The campfire at night was the only source of warmth, and windmills and waterwheels creaked and turned, powering the stone mills and looms. The air was thus exceptionally fresh, each breath like drinking from a mountain spring. Guns were never invented, but legend had it that some could "communicate with the energy of steel," these dreamers were called "Iron Summoners"—they believed that deep within the cold metal lay slumbering souls, which could be awakened with the right words. Now this is a lost song, a lullaby that children used to fall asleep to, dreaming of their shadows transforming into armored giants.

The artisans of the Star Federation had long since reached the limits of the laws of physics. They knew atoms existed, and even knew how to disintegrate them, but all of that was permanently sealed in a black box in the polar ice caps, a box even the Speaker himself dared not approach. No one knew exactly what was inside the black box, but all the rulers had reached a tacit agreement: that thing could never be opened. Some doors, once opened, can never be closed again.


It's important to note that psionic strength only represents short-term burst power and should never be used to directly measure actual combat strength. Someone with a very high psionic value might just be a cultivator who doesn't understand practical combat, while someone with an average value might be proficient in combat techniques and professional abilities. Unsophisticated cultivators only look at the numbers, while true sages observe the opponent's aura—they can roughly estimate a person's cultivation level through their aura. But this is only an estimation; when unsure, people avoid engaging in combat.
