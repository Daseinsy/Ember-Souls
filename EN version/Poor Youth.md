#  Poor Youth

The night was deep, but the lights in the square were even brighter than during the day. Today was the "Awakening Day" for the teenagers, and all those who had turned sixteen had to undergo an awakening ceremony here.
Qin Mo stood beneath the stone pillar, his palms sweating. The black water mirror before him floated silently in the air, like a fragment from the Mirror Realm, waiting to awaken his soul.
"Close your eyes and visualize." The elder's voice was deep and authoritative.
Qin Mo took a deep breath. In the darkness, it was as if a rift had been torn open in his mind, and strange lights and shadows emerged from the depths. At first, he only saw blurry outlines—mountains, rivers, blood, and fire. But soon, those outlines coalesced into a gigantic figure:
—A scaleless dragon, its eyes like molten gold, its body coiled around the barren land.
"How is this possible..." someone exclaimed in a low voice.
Qin Mo suddenly opened his eyes, a sharp pain shooting through his chest. He felt a burning sensation on his forehead, and in the water mirror, a golden mark was slowly emerging, like the echo of some ancient symbol in the world.
"Totem mark... Azure Dragon?" The elder's voice lost its composure for the first time.
A hush fell over the surroundings, followed by an uproar. Everyone knew that the Azure Dragon was not merely a personal totem, but rather—a symbol of the entire Federation's destiny.
Qin Mo had been staring at that water mirror for a long time.
The square was bustling with noise as children walked one by one to the mirror, closed their eyes, and waited for the totem to appear. Some quickly shone with light, clear lines etched on their foreheads; others remained motionless, and were led away by the elders. Each difference between light and darkness felt like the public unveiling of fate.
He suddenly remembered the beggar he had met on the street corner a few days earlier. The man's forehead was completely gray, the marks of his past blurred away; he was known as the "Lost Image Man." People kept their distance from him, as if he were contagious. He sat by the trash can, staring blankly at the passersby, muttering to himself, "Totems are illusions too."
Qin Mo dared not think about it further.
He looked down and saw that his palms were sweaty. Others say awakening is a "sacred moment," but he felt like he was being slowly dragged into a suffocating abyss. He was afraid—not afraid of failure, but afraid that what he saw would be different from what others saw.
It was his turn.
He walked to the water mirror, his heart pounding in his chest. The surrounding noise gradually faded away, leaving only his own breathing. He didn't close his eyes immediately, but stared at the unfamiliar face in the mirror: fifteen years old, thin, with messy eyebrows and eyes full of hesitation.
He suddenly thought: What if nothing is shown?
What kind of person will I become then? Will I be that beggar on the street corner, or an unknown shadow even less than a beggar?
He slowly closed his eyes.
Something is approaching in the darkness.
