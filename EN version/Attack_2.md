#  Attack

The bell rang, signaling the end of get out of class. The once quiet classroom instantly became lively. He stood by the window, looking up. The warm afternoon sun slanted into his eyes, as if shrouding him in a layer of ancient golden sand. The wind blew past his ears, carrying countless sounds from miles away. Ah, he frowned, his ears twitching, trying to lower his overly sensitive senses. He walked past his classmates with a cold expression, carrying a black backpack. Occasionally, he heard snippets of conversation: "Wow, what a strange person." Suddenly, a red alarm sounded, as if a blood-red shadow had been cast over the surrounding air. Then came screams, the sounds of buildings collapsing, and desks and chairs being smashed. A terrifying monster with a human head and a snake's body swept through the crowd, splattering blood everywhere. This city hadn't experienced such a violent demonic invasion in years. Although they had conducted drills as a precaution, those were just drills. The demonic invasion was particularly eerie, complex, and unpredictable, like a sudden, devastating attack while you were asleep or most relaxed. "Everyone, get to the shelter!" the teacher shouted through sonic booms, rushing to the front to obsessively resist the monster. Behind him, a group of students ran desperately, their faces filled with terror and confusion. The shadow of death seemed to have driven them mad. Friends quickly grabbed each other's hands and raced past. They channeled their psychic energy, instantly leaping past. Some collapsed from exhaustion, desperately crying for help. Some were lucky enough to encounter kind strangers who helped them; others were left behind and brutally torn apart by the monster's claws. After a while, the special operations force finally arrived . A figure crackling with blue lightning crashed into the monster, followed by a fire giant descending from the sky, surrounding it. A giant vine snake, its tongue flicking, coiled around the monster, halting its movement. The remaining students, seeing the situation was going well, surprisingly stopped running and stayed to watch the spectacle, until the special forces captain roared, "What are you standing there for? Want to go to the underworld to watch this?" The monster landed heavily, caught in a net by the special forces and dragged away. The previous incident was still chilling; the school held a memorial service. The surviving students, seeing their families rushing in in panic, wept and huddled together. Solemnly, how had the monster invaded without their knowledge? Why were there no preventative measures? How could they prevent a repeat of this tragedy? In a secluded corner, the protagonist stared intently at the magic circle on the ground, lost in thought. The monster had actually been very strong, but the protagonist had used a weakening spell on it when no one was watching, sealing its armor-piercing effect. Yes, it was armor-piercing. He remained hidden, using his psychic powers to create various accidents and save those he wanted to save. A conspiracy—he had only just transferred to this school and was unaware of its intricacies. He discovered it was ultimately a man-made sacrificial ritual. "Did you hear that? The wind told me the answer." But he just wanted a peaceful life. However, in his most desolate moment, his neighbor's sister, who had mysteriously died in an accident, helped him. His spiritual senses indicated something was amiss. The protagonist joined an expedition team—yes, he was an archaeologist, a dangerous and tiring field, though the pay might be high, it was mostly luck. But the protagonist always seemed to stumble upon unexpected treasures. The protagonist sat alone in the shadows, like a lonely little animal. "Hungry? Go home for dinner." He felt as if he possessed boundless power, but didn't know what to do with it. He felt nothing was meaningful, yet he still wanted to live, still wanted to protect everything—call it escapism if you will. On his way home, he walked through a secluded alley, sensing someone following him. "Great, it's you!" he thought, and randomly beat up the bad guys to punish evil. Kid, you've been playing dumb, but you've messed with me. You destroy the evidence, grab the money bag. You take down the weaklings, then the strongmen appear, and you launch another counterattack. A girl witnesses the incident, but instead of killing her, the protagonist places a veil of silence. No one is allowed in the lower levels, but the protagonist, disguised as a courtesan, enters. A Gold Membership, a chat group teeming with VIPs and powerful figures—a magical artifact, but the protagonist isn't interested. While out archaeological dig, he encounters a saintess. He's saved by her in the blink of an eye, and they become entangled. Ah, this cult seems a bit off. Saintesses mysteriously disappear, die, or are promoted, or seem to have been replaced, causing panic among those who know. Some even betrayed the cult and were burned alive? He discovers a blood pool within the cult, filled with the saintess's powerful spirit. Then the story takes another turn: it turns out all the saintesses throughout history are the same person, a parasitic entity.

The protagonist finds an elixir. He joins the Divine Sect, and they are now in a master-disciple relationship. However, the junior sister isn't the master's daughter, but rather the daughter of an old friend of his. He possesses the power to pacify the world with a single sword strike, earning him the title of Sword Demon, a Primordial Demon Realm expert. He needs to eat, but he can conjure food at will. His daily life is so ordinary, so mundane, so boring—eating, drinking, and relieving himself. However, some people have transformed themselves and don't have these worries. Some powerful beings will stop at nothing to prolong their lives. Physical decay is easily controlled—they can simply change bodies—but the spirit also suffers the five signs of decay, so they rely on absorbing spirits to extend their lives—a secret, evil art. The Nameless Society, for the sake of peace, consists of extreme idealists, yet they are also very rational. There's no intense anxiety; he's invincible from the start, but… The villainous force, the Burning Fire Temple, operates under high-pressure management. Its members choose to harm others to protect their own interests, yet they also promote justice. However, it does have positive effects, after all, it has achieved renewable fire technology. Do you know why it's fire? Why is it that places without fire are shrouded in darkness, filled with countless shadows and monsters, especially the sea, where bizarre creatures abound? Every settlement has a sun in the sky, and then it disappears; the sun's rest is nighttime. But the wilderness is utterly dark, devoid of any sun. Legend has it that long, long ago, there was a true sun, but it fell after Ragnarok. In truth, evil spirits are born of humanity itself; as long as humanity exists, evil spirits will appear—nature's attempt to destroy humanity. If the universe is so silent, isn't life a mistake, something that shouldn't exist? However, settlements cannot develop on their own, so they must interact with the outside world, leading to sacrifices and comparisons. Although the wilderness is fraught with danger, there are still relatively safe routes, known as the Firepaths, where fires burn along the way, forming a relatively safe network connecting various city-states. This path is also called the Firepath. Legend says that four sages sacrificed their lives for it: the Sage of Life, the Sage of Water, the Sage of Spirit… People always assume that evil spirits are inherently evil, but the reason they attack living beings so frantically is because of their innate madness. To alleviate this madness, they can only resort to bloodlust. And there's a strange phenomenon: after killing enough living beings, the evil spirit's bloodlust subsides, and it returns to the underworld. This phenomenon is called "sublimation and return." The evil spirit may reappear in a more frenzied form, but with greater power. Legend has it that there are seventy-two terrifying evil spirits that have caused catastrophes. These evil spirits are said to originate from the space between the underworld and the human world. There is a forbidden area inhabited by an awakened, intelligent evil spirit that possesses methods to prevent other evil spirits from attacking. This forbidden area also collaborates with groups of evil spirit masters. First, they create a sense of oppression, using an attack to emphasize the terror and unpredictability of the evil spirits. Then, the source of power is finite; the stronger the source, the safer the place. Therefore, people desperately try to climb into the inner city and the temple, where the powerful figures hold direct control over their lives. Meanwhile, evil spirits appear intermittently in remote small towns.
