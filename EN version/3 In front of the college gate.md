# 3 In front of the college gate

The plaza was paved with a single piece of bluish-white jade, right next to the academy gate, but we had not yet truly entered the academy. Voices surged from all directions—children's laughter, parents' admonitions, the rustling of feathers as spirit beasts flapped their wings, all blending into a low buzzing sound.

“I’ve heard that talented people are chosen by the gods.” A young man in fine clothes standing next to the young lady—her brother—looked at them with a complicated expression and sighed softly, “But what is talent? Spirituality is something that can’t be measured or quantified. In the end, we can only roughly measure a child’s potential by their spiritual energy level.”

The young man's voice gradually turned resentful: "I spend all my time in the inner city library, getting the best spiritual practitioners I can find to tutor me every day, yet my little sister beat me to it."

"Hey bro, can you stop complaining? It's so sore!" The young lady poked his waist hard.

The familiar scent of cedar drifted from afar. Xuejian had somehow walked up to him and was looking at the tightly closed golden gate in the distance. She said softly, "But there are always those who achieve great things later in life." Lin Jing didn't need to turn around to know that it was her—that cool and clean aura was almost the only "familiar" thing he could recognize in this unfamiliar city.

The young man paused for a moment. “Some people don’t even reach the ‘Primordial Mind’ until they are decades old or even in their twilight years,” Xuejian’s voice was very soft, as if she were talking to herself, “and then they travel swiftly up the ‘Stairway to Godhood’.” The young man scratched his head and smiled bitterly: “Yes, but that is the destiny of the chosen ones.”

High in the sky, several young men who looked like academy deacons rode spirit beasts resembling eagles, silently gliding across the square, their gazes steady and sharp.

Lin Jing sensed the surging spiritual light around her and gazed at the novel things she had never heard of before. In the distance, the outline of a massive academy was faintly visible, as if floating in a galaxy. Beside her, some of the higher-ups were attended by semi-transparent spirit servants, their outlines like human figures blurred in ink wash, their movements gentle and considerate, even the orientation of the teacup was precise when serving tea; not far away, several standardized spirit puppets were mechanically carrying luggage, their metal joints emitting fine clicking sounds, their faces all bearing the same standard peaceful smile, like silent servants molded from the same mold.

A beautiful single mother held the hand of a little girl. Their simple clothes contrasted sharply with the wealthy people of the inner city. Because the child was too young, the mother was allowed to accompany her child to the inner part of the temple according to special regulations, but was still not permitted to enter the academy itself.

Other parents nearby asked softly, "How old is the little girl this year?" The little girl replied timidly, "I'm nine years old." The people around sighed, "At nine years old, she can come to the academy. This child has a limitless future."

Lin Jing noticed that some audience members slightly smirked, then quickly composed themselves, revealing only polite smiles. They seemed to recall many examples of young people who achieved fame at a young age but eventually faded into obscurity. Spiritual power was merely an achievement at the moment, but life was a marathon, not a sprint.

Lin Jing stood alone in the bustling crowd, even the air around him seemed to carry a slight chill. He raised his hand and touched the left side of his chest; it felt empty—he couldn't tell if something was missing, or if it had never been there at all. The children around him were all led by adults, but he was all alone.

The little girl didn't go near anyone else, but timidly walked to his side and said, "Brother, it's so cool here." Her eyes were as clear as if they had been washed countless times. Lin Jing was slightly taken aback, looked down at her, and his lips moved, as if he didn't know how to respond. He had never known that standing next to him could make someone feel cool. He subconsciously looked at the back of his hand; nothing seemed amiss. But that childish remark was like a pebble thrown into a deep well, sinking without a sound, only feeling itself sinking deeper and deeper. He then said, "Perhaps it's because there are fewer people here."

At this moment, the morning dew had not yet dissipated, and the magnificent dome shimmered with a glassy hue, with sacred light descending from above.

A dignified yet gentle voice rang out: "Everyone, please don't be nervous. The following is the baptism procedure."

Immediately afterward, a massive magic circle appeared on the ground. The instant the circle lit up, pale golden specks of light, like a swarm of fireflies, rose from the cracks in the floor tiles, slowly rotating and ascending, like countless gentle hands cradling each person's ankles. Lin Jing felt a lightness beneath her feet, as if she were stepping on a layer of still water. A very faint buzzing sounded in her ears, like someone humming a wordless tune in the distance. The weight pressing on her chest silently dissipated with this sound, and the heaviness in her body seemed to evaporate slowly like dew after rain.

Then, specks of light fell from the sky, and the identity plaques on each child's chests began to glow in unison. These plaques were the identity credentials each citizen possessed from childhood, bound to each person's unique spiritual imprint, impossible to forge. Lin Jing glanced down at his own—a dull, metallic gray plaque engraved with a string of numbers he knew by heart, yet he always felt those numbers were like a label stuck to his skin, belonging to him, yet not entirely him. He reached out and touched it; the plaque was slightly warm, as if something was confirming his identity.

In the crowd, several elegantly dressed women gathered together, surrounded by spirit puppets with sharp expressions. They stretched their slightly plump bodies comfortably while discussing whose child had the purest spiritual light.

At this moment, the rules and precautions of school life, as well as their corresponding living space, naturally emerge in each child's mind. It is a blueprint for memory; even if it cannot be absorbed immediately, it can be recalled when needed.

Following the instructions, the crowd began to move slowly forward, like a gently flowing river.

The golden halo in the center of the magic circle gradually receded. Just then, the light in the air suddenly faded from golden to pale white. The warmth seemed to be choked and abruptly stopped, as if something had torn a thin crack open from the outside.

Lin Jing had only taken two steps when a chilling sensation, like rotting wood, suddenly shot up his spine from the soles of his feet. The cacophony of voices around him seemed distant and indistinct, as if separated by a layer of water. He instinctively stopped, and almost simultaneously, a twisted black shadow squeezed out from a suddenly appearing rift in the void, carrying a putrid stench, and lunged straight toward him.

A scream erupted like a ripped piece of cloth, throwing the surrounding crowd into chaos. Her mother was just a few steps away, head down, packing her bags. The shadowy figure appeared too fast; Lin Jing only had time to turn sideways and use his shoulder to pull the little girl who had been standing next to him towards her mother.

But as soon as the shadowy figure appeared, it was locked onto by several powerful auras and froze like an insect nailed to the air. The nearby deacon simply waved his hand, and the shadowy figure was suppressed to the ground. Then, several golden chains reached out from the sky and melted it into ashes.

A few seconds of silence fell over the plaza; even the sound of spirit beasts flapping their wings ceased. Only then did someone belatedly realize what was happening, shouting, "An evil spirit—an evil spirit has invaded!" "This is the academy gate! How could an evil spirit dare to appear here?"

"Don't panic." That dignified yet gentle voice rang out again, carrying an indescribable soothing power that made people unconsciously relax.

The academy's administrators, observing everything through their clairvoyant vision, whispered, "This indicates that more than one spiritually powerful child is being targeted by evil spirits." A moment later, someone reported in a low voice, "None of the students are tainted by evil energy; they can be released."

There were no more pleasantries. The golden academy gates suddenly opened to both sides, like an eagle spreading its wings, creating invisible waves of air. The children felt as if they were locked in by a vortex of airflow, as if an unseen hand was lifting them up and carrying them through the gates. The surrounding families stood still; the academy was a sacred place, and outsiders were not allowed to enter at will.

The elegantly dressed young man waved weakly at his younger sister, a thin layer of envy on his face.

The little girl in mid-air looked back at her mother, her eyes glistening with tears: "I'll miss home, I'll miss Mom." The mother stopped in place, stretched out her arms to her, and seemed to call out something. Her eyes held reluctance, determination, and hope washed away by tears.

Lin Jing lagged behind, glancing back at the scattered crowd in the square, then at his side. The faint warmth from when the little girl had approached still seemed to linger on his sleeve. He couldn't tell if it was left by the little girl or seeping from deep within himself.

He withdrew his gaze, his limbs curled up like a sleeping infant, letting the pulling wind sweep him inside. In the dark golden light, the massive outline of the distant temple loomed in and out of sight; the god who almost never showed himself remained there, maintaining his utmost mystery.
